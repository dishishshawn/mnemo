# Pricing models for AI study and flashcard apps on the iOS App Store (as of 14 Oct 2026), and the viability of a one-time $2.99 BYOK app

Research date: 14 October 2026. Every price below carries the date of the source it came from. Where the only available sources are third-party price trackers rather than the vendor, that is flagged. "Estimate" marks modelled rather than measured numbers.

## Key question 1: What pricing models do the leading AI study / flashcard apps use, and at what price points?

### Takeaway
Every AI-first study app found (Thea, Knowt, Quizlet, StudyFetch, Turbolearn, Mindgrasp, Brainscape, RemNote, Mochi) is freemium plus subscription, with monthly prices clustered at $5–$10 and annual plans at $35–$45 (Quizlet Plus $35.99/yr, Knowt Premium $35/yr, Quizlet Plus Unlimited $44.99/yr). The only one-time-paid apps in the space are the classic, non-AI flashcard tools (AnkiMobile $24.99, Flashcards Deluxe $4.99, Studies $4.99).

## Key question 2: Which successful study/flashcard apps are one-time paid, and what is known about their revenue, downloads or rank?

### Takeaway
AnkiMobile ($24.99) is the #1 top-paid Education app on the US App Store and is the only one-time-paid study app with a publicly stated business rationale: its developer tested lower prices and found they reduced total income. Flashcards Deluxe ($4.99) sits at #27 top-paid Education. No published revenue or download figures exist for either.

### Cited Findings
- AnkiMobile: $24.99, #1 in Education top-paid, 2.4K ratings, 4.0 stars (fetched 14 Oct 2026) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ankimobile-flashcards/id373493387)

"They have tested lower prices and found that cutting the price reduced overall income, since spaced repetition apps are a niche market compared with mass-appeal games." No sales or promo codes planned; donations alone would not sustain the project; one purchase covers up to five devices plus Family Sharing — [Anki docs FAQ](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/why-does-ankimobile-cost-more-than-a-typical-mobile-app.md)

- Studies: $4.99 iOS one-time, no IAPs — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/studies/id986066259)
- Halide (camera, not study) built its reputation on a $6 one-time price before moving to $36/yr — [Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/app-revenue-potential)

### Inferences
- The top-paid Education chart in the US is led by a $24.99 app with only 2.4K lifetime ratings, which suggests the paid Education chart is thin; a well-reviewed $2.99–$4.99 app could plausibly chart in top-paid Education with modest volume (estimate; no download data to confirm).

## Key question 3: Distribution of paid price points in Education; is $2.99 common; paid-upfront vs freemium performance in 2025–2026

### Takeaway
Roughly 90% of App Store apps are free; of the ~10% paid, the $1–$4.99 band is by far the most populated (5.2% of all apps vs 2.0% under $1 and 1.7% at $5–$9.99), so $2.99 sits in the modal paid band. Education is the #2 category by app count with a paid share estimated at 3–9% depending on tracker. RevenueCat 2026 shows hard paywalls convert 10.7% of downloads to paid vs 2.1% for freemium, but Education subscription apps earn only $0.30 revenue per install at day 14.

### Cited Findings
- App Pricing Lab 2025 report: 89.9% of apps free (122.8K). Paid bands: $0.01–$0.99 2.7K (2.0%); $1–$4.99 7.1K (5.2%); $5–$9.99 2.3K (1.7%); $10–$24.99 1.0K (0.8%); $25+ 432 (0.3%). Average paid price $6.50. Education: 10.1K apps on the App Store (#2 by count), 1.7K with IAP. Daily crawls; retrieved 2026 — [App Pricing Lab 2025 market report](https://apppricinglab.com/reports/market/2025)
- App Pricing Lab 2026 snapshots: Education 10.2K apps with 744 paid (7%), average paid price $279 (skewed by expensive professional/exam apps); another snapshot 9% paid, avg $220 — [App Pricing Lab free vs paid 2026](https://apppricinglab.com/insights/free-vs-paid); [App Pricing Lab Education](https://apppricinglab.com/reports/education)
- 42matters (via ElectroIQ): paid apps are 4.66% of the whole iOS App Store in 2025; Google Play Education 239,835 free vs 6,820 paid (~2.8%) — [ElectroIQ App Store stats 2026](https://electroiq.com/stats/app-store-revenue-statistics/)
- Paid apps generated only ~$6B globally in 2024, a small share of total app spend — [Adapty: how much do apps make](https://adapty.io/blog/how-much-money-do-apps-make/)
- Appfigures "Paid Apps are Dead" (Sept 2021): share of paid apps at its lowest; "If you're still holding on to a pay upfront model, it's time to stop doing that" — [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20210924?f=3)
- RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 (115K+ apps, $16B+ revenue), Education cuts: median yearly price $44.99 (highest of any category); median monthly $9.99 (tied highest); download-to-trial D30 6.5%; annual plans 59–66% of subscriptions sold; only 28.5% of download-to-paid conversions on Day 0 (lowest); D14 revenue per install $0.30; yearly realized LTV per payer $22.82 — [RevenueCat SoSA 2026 Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)
- RevenueCat 2026 all-category: hard paywall converts 10.7% of downloads to paid at D35 vs 2.1% freemium; D14 RPI $2.32 vs $0.27; global median download-to-paid 2.0%; median annual RLTV per payer $23; trial-to-paid 37.4% for 5–9 day trials, 42.5% for 17–32 days; ~14,700 new subscription apps launched in January 2026 (77% iOS); apps launched 2025 or later generate 3% of subscription revenue — [RevenueCat SoSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- RevenueCat 2026: top 25% of apps grew MRR 80% YoY, top 10% grew 306%, bottom quartile shrank 33% — [PPC Land on RevenueCat 2026](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)
- Adapty 2026 benchmark (indie-heavy sample, as cited): hard paywall 12.11% median download-to-paid; freemium + subscription 2.18%; trial-to-paid 8–12% good, 15–25% great — [Autoappnotes, May 2026](https://autoappnotes.substack.com/p/ios-buyout-vs-subscription-a-4-app)

### Inferences
- $2.99 is squarely in the most common paid band ($1–$4.99 holds ~52% of all paid apps), so it is a normal tier, not an outlier.
- A hard paywall (pay up front) converts roughly 5x better per download than freemium, but Education's $44.99 median annual price means a converted subscriber is worth ~15x a $2.99 sale; a one-time app needs ~8–15x the paying-user count to match a subscription peer's revenue (estimate derived from the figures above).
- The RevenueCat Education RPI of $0.30 at D14 is useful as a benchmark: a $2.99 app at a 10% hard-paywall conversion would net about $0.25/download at 15% commission, i.e. comparable to freemium Education apps on the same per-install basis (estimate).

### Gaps
- No tracker publishes a per-price-point histogram ($0.99 vs $1.99 vs $2.99 vs $4.99); only bands.
- No Appfigures or Sensor Tower 2025–2026 Education-specific paid-vs-freemium performance data was found.
- RevenueCat covers only subscription apps, so it has no benchmark for one-time-paid Education apps.

## Key question 4: Apple's cut, the developer fee, and 2025–2026 changes relevant to a $2.99 app

### Takeaway
A developer under $1M/yr in proceeds qualifies for the 15% Small Business Program rate on paid apps and IAP; otherwise 30%. The Apple Developer Program costs $99/yr. The US anti-steering litigation affects only external-link purchases and is unresolved (Supreme Court granted cert June 2026); it has no bearing on a paid-up-front app sold through Apple's own checkout.

### Cited Findings
- Small Business Program: 15% commission on paid apps and IAP for developers with up to $1M USD in proceeds in the prior calendar year (new developers qualify); proceeds are "sales net of Apple's commission and certain taxes and adjustments"; exceeding $1M moves you to the standard rate for future sales; re-qualify the year after falling back under; EU alternative-terms developers and post-year-1 subscriptions get 10%; reduced rate takes effect 15 days after the end of the fiscal month in which enrollment is approved — [Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)
- Program announced Nov 2020, live 1 Jan 2021 — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201118005376/en/Apple-Announces-App-Store-Small-Business-Program)
- Apple Developer Program: 99 USD per membership year; fee waivers only for nonprofits, accredited educational institutions and government entities — [Apple enrollment](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- UK CMA call for evidence (2026) confirms the 30% headline / 15% small-developer structure still in force — [UK government PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69cce7e0eafd66b876458b25/Call_for_evidence_.pdf)
- Epic v. Apple: district court found Apple in contempt on 30 Apr 2025 and barred any commission on external-link purchases in the US; Apple had tried a 12–27% commission on linked-out purchases. Ninth Circuit (11 Dec 2025) affirmed contempt, found the 27% fee had a "prohibitive economic effect", but rejected an unconditional ban on any link-out commission and remanded that question. Supreme Court granted certiorari 30 June 2026 — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/06/30/high-court-grants-cert-in-apples-challenge-to-ninth-circuit-contempt-ruling-in-app-store-dispute/); [MacRumors June 2026](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/11/apple-fights-back-against-epic/); [Courthouse News order PDF](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/apple-v-epic-games-usdc-ndca-order-contempt.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a $2.99 paid app, the anti-steering rulings are irrelevant: there is no external purchase to steer to, and paid-up-front apps cannot use link-out at all. The only live variable is enrolling in the Small Business Program (which a new developer should do before the first sale, since the rate is not retroactive).
- Net per sale (estimate, US storefront where the listed price excludes sales tax and Apple remits it as marketplace facilitator): $2.99 × 0.85 = $2.54 at 15%; $2.99 × 0.70 = $2.09 at 30%. In VAT countries Apple deducts VAT from the local tier price before commission, so net is lower (e.g. a EUR 2.99 tier at ~20% VAT yields roughly EUR 2.12 at 15%). Income tax on proceeds depends on the developer's jurisdiction and was not researched.
- Break-even on the $99/yr fee alone: ~39 sales/yr at 15%, ~48 at 30% (estimate). Gemini API costs are zero to the developer under BYOK.

### Gaps
- Status of the remanded "reasonable commission" on link-outs is unknown as of Oct 2026; not relevant to a paid app anyway.
- Apple's published price-tier table (which confirms $2.99 as a standard tier) was not fetched directly; the tier's existence is evidenced by apps listed at $2.99 in the autoappnotes post and the App Pricing Lab bands.

## Key question 5: What monetization commentators say about $0.99–$4.99 one-time prices today, and the counter-arguments

### Takeaway
The dominant industry view since 2021 is that pay-upfront is a shrinking model with front-loaded revenue, no trial mechanism and an LTV capped at the sticker price; the counter-argument, gaining traction in 2026 vendor data, is that subscription fatigue produces one-star reviews and Day-7 churn for low-utility apps, and that one-time pricing is "mathematically superior" when there are no server costs. Both sides agree that the deciding factor is whether the app carries ongoing inference/server cost, which a BYOK app does not.

### Cited Findings
- Appfigures (2021): "Paid Apps are Dead"; move to IAP or subscriptions — [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20210924?f=3)
- Cult of Mac: the paid-app market is "all but dead"; Apple does not let developers charge for updates, so paid buyers get all future versions free — [Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/?p=451594)
- Indie developer (2019): paid-app revenue arrives in bursts around launches and updates, making sustained income hard; commenter: the problem is that the App Store makes trials for paid apps difficult, not pay-once itself — [Michael Tsai blog, 2019](https://mjtsai.com/blog/?p=26724)
- Apple's refund API lets only the customer request a refund, not the developer (Tweetbot/Twitterrific case, 2023) — [Michael Tsai blog, 2023](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2023/03/02/tweetbot-and-twitterrific-subscription-cliff)
- Paid apps keep high margins but LTV caps at the purchase price; the model works only with a steady stream of new buyers from ASO; typical paid-upfront range $2–$10 — [Forasoft 2026](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/app-revenue-potential); $3–$15 range per [The Swift Kit 2026](https://theswiftk.it.com/blog/how-to-monetize-ios-app-indie-developer); "$3.99 to $9.99 impulse sweet spot" per [Superappp 2026](https://www.superappp.com/blog/how-to-make-money-from-an-app)
- Stora (Apr 2026, vendor blog citing Adapty 2026): one-time purchases making a comeback as users cut subscriptions; "Slapping a subscription on a calculator or a QR scanner is a fast path to one-star reviews and Day 7 churn" — [Stora blog](https://stora.sh/blog/2026-04-06-subscription-fatigue-one-time-purchases-comeback)
- Autoappnotes (6 May 2026) worked model: 1,000 downloads × 12% hard-paywall conversion × $2.99 × 0.7 = $251 vs subscription 1,000 × 2.18% × $1.99 × 5 months × 0.7 = $152; concludes one-time IAP is "mathematically superior" for utility apps and subscription wins only with continuous server costs, daily-habit use or a LiveOps team. Suggested health thresholds: Day-30 conversion ~5% confirms the model, ~2% means paywall needs work; refund rate under 2% target, over 5% suggests misleading description; restore-purchase rate 5–10% normal — [Autoappnotes](https://autoappnotes.substack.com/p/ios-buyout-vs-subscription-a-4-app)
- RevenueCat-based figure: median monthly revenue for apps at least one year old is under $50; only 17.2% of apps ever exceed $1K/month — [The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/news/most-subscription-apps-don-t-make-bank)

### Inferences
- The "paid app graveyard" argument rests on (a) no trial, (b) no recurring revenue to fund servers, (c) discovery favouring free apps. For a BYOK app, (b) largely disappears; (a) and (c) remain. A free-download-plus-$2.99-one-time-unlock (non-consumable IAP) captures the hard-paywall conversion math while allowing a trial, and is the structure Pal Chat, OpenCat and the autoappnotes apps actually use.
- No source directly measured discoverability penalties for paid-upfront apps in 2025–2026; this part of the argument remains assertion rather than data.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 published refund-rate data for $0.99–$4.99 paid apps was found; only the autoappnotes rule-of-thumb targets.
- No quantitative evidence on whether paid-upfront apps are shown less in App Store search/featuring.

## Key question 6: How do existing bring-your-own-key AI apps on the App Store price themselves?

### Takeaway
BYOK AI chat clients on iOS are almost uniformly free to download with a one-time or low-cost Pro unlock in the $4–$10 range (OpenCat Pro $9.99, Pal Chat Pro $6.99, OpenCat Cloud $3.99), or entirely free/open source (Enchanted, Chatbox BYOK tier). Desktop/web BYOK tools charge higher one-time licences (TypingMind $39–$99 once; Msty $349 lifetime). None found uses a mandatory subscription for BYOK use.

### Cited Findings
- OpenCat (iOS): free with IAPs: Cloud AI $3.99, OpenCat Pro $9.99, tokens package $5.99; supports BYOK, its own cloud and local models — [App Store: OpenCat](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/opencat-ai-chat-agent-mcp/id6445999201)

### Inferences
- The established BYOK convention on iOS is "free download + one-time Pro unlock", not pay-upfront; a $2.99 unlock would be the cheapest Pro tier among the apps found (OpenCat $9.99, Pal Chat $6.99).

- Hollo and Mela-type apps were not located in search results.

## Key question 7: Student willingness to pay and sentiment toward paywalls

### Takeaway
Student AI use is near-universal (42% weekly in Tyton 2025; "nearly all" in HEPI 2026; 54% of US teens for schoolwork per Pew), but paying is rare: a 2024 engineering-student survey found 7.1% paying for LLM chatbots, and an aggregated 2026 statistic claims ~9 in 10 university students think AI tools should cost under $10/month. Quizlet's 2022 paywall produced documented student backlash and migration to free alternatives such as Knowt.

### Cited Findings
- Tyton Partners Time for Class 2025 (3,300+ students, instructors, administrators across 900+ US institutions): ~42% of students use generative AI weekly vs 30% of instructors — [Tyton Partners](https://tytonpartners.com/time-for-class-2025/)

### Inferences
- Students' stated ceiling (<$10/mo) and actual payment rate (~7%) suggest a $2.99 one-time price is well inside tolerance; the obstacle is not price but the habit of expecting study tools free, as the Quizlet reaction shows.
- The Quizlet episode shows students will migrate for free alternatives; a $2.99 app competes against free tiers of Thea, Knowt and Quizlet, not against their paid tiers.

## Key question 8: What $2.99 nets per sale, and how many sales indie education apps make per month

### Takeaway
At the 15% Small Business rate a US $2.99 sale nets about $2.54 (about $2.09 at 30%), before income tax. Published indie benchmarks are bleak: median app revenue under $50/month a year after launch, with only ~17% ever passing $1K/month; the one detailed 2026 one-time-pricing model found projects $14–$87/month per app at 200–500 downloads/month and 5% conversion, rising to ~$300–$1,200 at 1,500–2,500 downloads and 8–12% conversion.

### Cited Findings
- Commission: 15% under Small Business Program, 30% standard; proceeds net of commission and "certain taxes" — [Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)
- Developer fee $99/yr — [Apple enrollment](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- Median monthly revenue for apps ≥1 year old under $50; 17.2% ever exceed $1K/month (RevenueCat data) — [The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/news/most-subscription-apps-don-t-make-bank)
- Another guide attributes to a 2025 RevenueCat survey a median indie iOS app earning under $500/month, top 5% above $10K/month, top 1% above $50K — [Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/app-revenue-potential); the discrepancy with the <$50 figure reflects different populations (all apps vs surveyed indies) and is unresolved
- Autoappnotes model (May 2026): $2.99 one-time nets $2.09 at 30%; conservative scenario 200–500 downloads/month at 5% conversion yields $14–$87/month per app; optimistic 1,000–2,500 downloads at 8–12% yields $111–$1,196/month — [Autoappnotes](https://autoappnotes.substack.com/p/ios-buyout-vs-subscription-a-4-app)
- One developer's Medium post reports a launch earning about $2 total — [Medium](https://medium.com/@1qazster/can-indie-developers-still-make-money-i-tested-it-with-my-own-app-3340251320dd)
- Indie roundup (self-reported, weak): median indie app under $50/month; ~17% over $1K; ~5% over $10K — [Froxi 2026](https://froxi.ai/blog/how-much-money-do-indie-apps-actually-make-in-2026)
- RevenueCat Education benchmark for comparison: $0.30 revenue per install at D14; yearly realized LTV per payer $22.82 — [RevenueCat SoSA 2026 Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)

### Inferences
- Net per sale (estimate): $2.54 at 15%, $2.09 at 30%, US storefront, before the developer's income tax. 39 sales/year covers the $99 fee at 15%.
- To reach $500/month net at $2.54/sale requires ~197 sales/month; at a 10% hard-paywall conversion that is ~2,000 downloads/month, which is above the "conservative" indie scenario and in line with the "optimistic" one in the only detailed model found (estimate).
- Because BYOK removes inference cost, every dollar after the $99 fee is margin; the $2.99 model is viable as a low-maintenance side project but, on the published medians, not as a primary income.

### Gaps
- No postmortem with actual (not modelled) monthly sales figures for a $0.99–$4.99 Education app in 2024–2026 was found on Indie Hackers, HN or Reddit within the search budget.
- Download volumes for comparable indie study apps are not public; the autoappnotes figures are projections, not results.
- Tax treatment (US state sales tax remitted by Apple vs VAT deducted pre-commission abroad; developer income tax) was inferred from Apple's wording, not from a dedicated fetched source.

# App Store review, legal and platform constraints for shipping Mnemo (Capacitor-wrapped Vite PWA, BYOK Gemini, E2E sync) as a paid iOS app

Research date: 2026-10-14. Apple's App Review Guidelines page shows "Last Updated: June 8, 2026"; Google's Gemini API Additional Terms are "Effective March 23, 2026" (page stamp "Last updated 2026-04-28 UTC"). All guideline quotes below are from the live pages on the research date unless noted. "Confirmed rule" = quoted from Apple/Google primary text; "anecdote" = developer-forum or blog report.

Headline: there are two blockers that are not about App Review at all and sit above everything else. (1) Google's Gemini API Additional Terms forbid using the API in any app "directed towards or is likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18" and say the API is "not for consumer use". A student-targeted consumer app whose users paste a Gemini key is in direct tension with both clauses. (2) Apple's 3.1.1 has been applied, at least once in writing, to a paid BYOK app on the ground that "the app uses API keys to unlock or enable functionality". Everything else (4.2 web-wrapper risk, copycat/metadata rules, privacy label, age rating, export compliance) is manageable with concrete changes listed under each heading.

## 1. Guideline 4.2 Minimum Functionality for Capacitor / WKWebView apps

### Takeaway
Apple's 4.2 text targets apps that are "a repackaged website"; Capacitor as a technology is not itself banned, but reviewers judge what they experience, and recent (Jan 2026) forum evidence shows that bolting on Core Location, share sheet or push is not reliably enough if the core flow still looks like a web page. Bundle the Vite build inside the binary (never `server.url` to a remote site), make the app work offline, and describe native integrations in plain language in the review notes.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed rule, 4.2 intro (verbatim): "Your app should include features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website. If your app is not particularly useful, unique, or 'app-like,' it doesn't belong on the App Store. If your App doesn't provide some sort of lasting entertainment value or adequate utility, it may not be accepted." — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 4.2.2 (verbatim): "Other than catalogs, apps shouldn't primarily be marketing materials, advertisements, web clippings, content aggregators, or a collection of links." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 4.2.3 (verbatim): "(i) Your app should work on its own without requiring installation of another app to function. (ii) If your app needs to download additional resources in order to function on initial launch, disclose the size of the download and prompt users before doing so." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 4.2.6 (verbatim): "Apps created from a commercialized template or app generation service will be rejected unless they are submitted directly by the provider of the app's content." (Not applicable to a hand-built Capacitor app, but reviewers sometimes cite it against wrapper-generator output.) — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 2.5.6: apps that browse the web must use WebKit; WKWebView-based Capacitor apps comply. — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 4.0 Design intro (verbatim): "...the following are minimum standards for approval to the App Store... Apps that stop working or offer a degraded experience may be removed from the App Store at any time." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Anecdote (Jan 2026, Apple Developer Forums): a Capacitor limo-booking app with custom Swift plugins (Core Location, CLGeocoder reverse geocoding, clipboard, share sheet, Apple Maps deep links) was rejected twice under 4.2; the developer paraphrased Apple as saying the experience was "not sufficiently differentiated from web browsing" and that features "such as Core Location or sharing alone are not robust enough". The booking/payment flow ran inside a third-party web page (RideBits). No Apple staff reply and no recorded resolution. A community reply argued 4.2 "concerns what the reviewer experiences, not the list of plugins". — [Apple Developer Forums thread 812889](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812889)
- Anecdote (Ionic Forum): "the technology doesn't matter" — loading a website through Capacitor or Cordova still gets rejected; the forum member paraphrases Apple as wanting "app like visual design, provide integration into device features, and be accessible without internet". — [Ionic Forum: Apple §4.2 Minimum Functionality](https://forum.ionicframework.com/t/apple-4-2-minimum-functionality/189688)
- Anecdote/vendor: multiple blogs and GitHub issues converge on the same recipe: bundle built web assets in the binary rather than pointing `server.url` at a live site; add offline support, push, haptics, share sheet, native navigation; write plain-language review notes with a demo account. — [Capgo: How to put a web app on the App Store](https://capgo.app/blog/how-to-put-a-web-app-on-the-app-store/); [Capgo capacitor-app-store skill](https://github.com/Cap-go/capgo-skills/blob/main/skills/capacitor-app-store/SKILL.md); [MobiLoud on WebView wrappers](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/app-store-review-guidelines-webview-wrapper); [technetexperts on 4.2](https://www.technetexperts.com/guideline-4-2-minimum-functionality/)
- Anecdote (HN, 2021): commenter lists Sign in with Apple, Apple Pay and HealthKit-style integrations as the kind of iOS-specific functionality that helps a wrapper pass. — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29043216)
- Confirmed rule, 4.3(b) Spam (verbatim): "Don't submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available. Opportunistically creating variants of existing app categories or popular apps degrades App Store discovery..." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- Mnemo already has offline-first IndexedDB storage, no remote `server.url` dependency, a penguin mascot/animations, dictation, audio podcast playback and encrypted sync. Those are stronger 4.2 signals than the rejected limo app had, because the core study loop runs on-device rather than inside someone else's web page. The weakest point is that the UI is identical to the PWA; a native tab bar, haptics (Capacitor Haptics), share-sheet export of study guides, local notifications for spaced-repetition reminders, and a home-screen widget would be cheap differentiators.
- 4.3(b) ("indistinguishable from what's already widely available") is the quieter risk for a self-described recreation; the 4.1 copycat section (below) is the louder one.

### Gaps
- I found no documented case in 2024-2026 of a Capacitor app that was rejected under 4.2 and then approved after adding specific native features, with Apple's acceptance text quoted. All "how to fix" material is vendor or community advice.
- Apple does not publish a checklist of what counts as "app-like"; the "15-minute review" and similar claims in vendor blogs are unverifiable.

## 2. Guideline 4.1 Copycats, 5.2 IP, 2.3.7 metadata, and the "Mnemo"/"Thea" names

### Takeaway
Apple's copycat rule is about copying "name or UI" and passing it off as your own, and about using another developer's "icon, brand, or product name" in your icon or name; reproducing a feature set with your own code, assets and name is not what 4.1 describes, but 4.3(b) and 5.2.1 ("copycat representations, names, or metadata") give reviewers latitude. Naming Thea in the App Store description/subtitle/keywords collides with 2.3.7 (subtitles "should not ... reference other apps"; don't pack metadata with "popular app names"). "Mnemo" is already used by a live free flashcard/spaced-repetition app in the Education category (Keiichirou Hayashi, 2023), plus several other Mnemo-named apps; I found no USPTO software-class registration for MNEMO either way.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed rule, 4.1(a) (verbatim): "Come up with your own ideas. We know you have them, so make yours come to life. Don't simply copy the latest popular app on the App Store, or make some minor changes to another app's name or UI and pass it off as your own. In addition to risking an intellectual property infringement claim, it makes the App Store harder to navigate and just isn't fair to your fellow developers." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 4.1(b) (verbatim): "Submitting apps which impersonate other apps or services is considered a violation of the Developer Code of Conduct and may result in removal from the Apple Developer Program." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 4.1(c) (verbatim): "You cannot use another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name, without approval from the developer." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 5.2.1 (verbatim): "Don't use protected third-party material such as trademarks, copyrighted works, or patented ideas in your app without permission, and don't include misleading, false, or copycat representations, names, or metadata in your app bundle or developer name. Apps should be submitted by the person or legal entity that owns or has licensed the intellectual property and other relevant rights." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 5.2.2 (verbatim): "If your app uses, accesses, monetizes access to, or displays content from a third-party service, ensure that you are specifically permitted to do so under the service's terms of use. Authorization must be provided upon request." (This is the hook under which Apple can ask you to prove Google's terms allow your Gemini usage; see section 4.) — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 2.3.7 (verbatim): "Choose a unique app name, assign keywords that accurately describe your app, and don't try to pack any of your metadata with trademarked terms, popular app names, pricing information, or other irrelevant phrases just to game the system. App names must be limited to 30 characters... App subtitles ... must follow our standard metadata rules and should not include inappropriate content, reference other apps, or make unverifiable product claims. Apple may modify inappropriate keywords at any time or take other appropriate steps to prevent abuse." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 2.3.8 (verbatim excerpt): "Remember to ensure your metadata, including app name and icons ... are similar to avoid creating confusion." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Thea's App Store listing: "Thea: Study Smart", subtitle "AI study tool you can trust", developer "Thea Study", seller Theaai Corporation, Education, age rating 13+ (infrequent medical/treatment info, alcohol/tobacco/drug references, guns/weapons), free with IAP (Monthly $12.00, Yearly $48.00), copyright "© 2023-2026 Theaai Corporation". Features: upload-based study kits, adaptive questions, flashcards with spaced repetition, practice tests, summaries/study guides, 80+ languages, educator tools. Privacy label declares Location, Contact info, User content, Identifiers, Usage data, Diagnostics linked to the user. — [apps.apple.com Thea: Study Smart](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/thea-study-smart/id6742800289)
- Thea corporate facts: Theaai Corporation, Arlington VA, founded by Baker Bruce with Anthony Bruce as co-founder/CEO; free for students with a premium tier Thea+. — [theastudy.com/llms.txt](https://theastudy.com/llms.txt)
- An existing App Store app named exactly "Mnemo" (id6463660490) by Keiichirou Hayashi: Education, 4+, free, iPhone/iPad/Mac/visionOS, English and Japanese, released 08/31/2023, last version 1.0.7 on 05/17/2024; it is a spaced-repetition flashcard app ("stores what you learn each day and prompts you to review items just as you're about to forget them"), with shareable decks and quizzes. — [apps.apple.com Mnemo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mnemo/id6463660490)
- Other live apps with Mnemo in the name: "Mnemo Remember" (caregivers/seniors, id6680171876), "Mnemo: Memory Card Game" (id6752640768), "mNemo — научись помнить всё" (mnemonics trainer, id1546896959), "MyMnemo" (French "digital memory" app with AI import, id6717610290), "Mnemosine" (id6742428134). — [App Store search results](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mnemo-remember/id6680171876); [Mnemo: Memory Card Game](https://apps.apple.com/py/app/mnemo-memory-card-game/id6752640768); [mNemo](https://apps.apple.com/py/app/mnemo-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%83%D1%87%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%8C-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%8C-%D0%B2%D1%81%D1%91/id1546896959); [MyMnemo](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/mymnemo/id6717610290)
- USPTO: web search surfaced only MNEMO JETS (Mnemo Therapeutics, Class 005 pharmaceuticals, registered), MNEMONIC (&Tradition A/S, registered) and MNEMOSYNE (Maruman, Class 016); no record for a standalone MNEMO software mark appeared, and TrademarkElite's search page could not be fetched. — [gleanmark MNEMO JETS](https://gleanmark.com/trademark/uspto-79345490); [TrademarkElite MNEMONIC](https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/79323170/MNEMONIC)
- No trademark filing or registration for "Thea"/theastudy.com surfaced in web search; WebCatalog's "not affiliated" disclaimer is a third-party boilerplate, not evidence of a registration. — [search results summary via theastudy.com and webcatalog](https://webcatalog.io/en/apps/thea)

### Inferences
- "Inspired by Thea, not affiliated" in the in-app About screen is low risk; the same phrase in the App Store subtitle or keywords is a 2.3.7 problem on the face of the text ("reference other apps", "popular app names"). Keep competitor names out of App Store Connect metadata; a neutral description of features is safe.
- Because Mnemo is a deliberate feature-for-feature recreation and the developer says so publicly, 4.3(b)/4.1(a) risk is real if a reviewer compares screenshots to Thea's. Distinct icon, colour scheme, mascot and layout (which the current one-screen Smart Study redesign already moves toward) are the mitigations Apple's text points at.
- App Store names must be unique per storefront in practice; "Mnemo" alone is already taken by a live Education app with the same spaced-repetition concept, so App Store Connect will almost certainly refuse the bare name and a trademark examiner would see a likelihood-of-confusion issue in Class 9/42 if the earlier developer ever files. Plan on a distinguishing name ("Mnemo Study", "Mnemo AI", or a different word) and check App Store Connect name availability early.

### Gaps
- I could not run a live USPTO TESS/TSDR or TrademarkElite query (JS-only interfaces); the absence of a MNEMO software mark is "not found in web search", not a cleared search. Same for THEA in Classes 9/41/42 by Theaai Corporation.
- No Apple rejection text citing 4.1 against an openly acknowledged "recreation" was found for 2024-2026.

## 3. Bring-your-own-API-key (BYOK) apps: 3.1.1, 3.1.3, 2.1, demo key, and linking to aistudio.google.com

### Takeaway
BYOK AI clients are on the App Store today (Pal Chat, OpenCat), but Apple has in writing rejected a paid BYOK app under 3.1.1 with "the app uses API keys to unlock or enable functionality" (Sept 2024, unresolved publicly). The safest shape is: the paid app is fully usable without a key for everything non-AI, the key field is presented as connecting the user's own existing Google account/service (3.1.3(b)-style "access content acquired elsewhere"), and App Review gets a working Gemini key in the review notes under 2.1(a). Linking users to aistudio.google.com to obtain a key is not a purchase link (Google's key is free to create) and so does not trigger 3.1.1(a); the May 2025 US-storefront change makes external links non-fatal in the US anyway, but the rest of the world still follows the old rule.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed rule, 3.1.1 (verbatim): "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app, (by way of example: subscriptions, in-game currencies, game levels, access to premium content, or unlocking a full version), you must use in-app purchase. Apps may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as license keys, augmented reality markers, QR codes, cryptocurrencies and cryptocurrency wallets, etc." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 3.1.1(a) (verbatim): "Developers may apply for entitlements to provide a link in their app to a website the developer owns or maintains responsibility for in order to purchase digital content or services. These entitlements are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 3.1.3 (verbatim): "The following apps may use purchase methods other than in-app purchase. Apps in this section cannot, within the app, encourage users to use a purchasing method other than in-app purchase, except for apps on the United States storefront and as set forth in 3.1.1(a) and 3.1.3(a). Developers can send communications outside of the app to their user base about purchasing methods other than in-app purchase." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 3.1.3(b) Multiplatform Services (verbatim): "Apps that operate across multiple platforms may allow users to access content, subscriptions, or features they have acquired in your app on other platforms or your web site, including consumable items in multi-platform games, provided those items are also available as in-app purchases within the app." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 3.1.3(f) Free Stand-alone Apps: free companion apps to paid web tools (VoIP, cloud storage, email, web hosting) need no IAP if there is no purchasing in the app and no purchase calls to action. — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 2.1(a) (verbatim excerpt): "...include demo account info (and turn on your back-end service!) if your app includes a login. If you are unable to provide a demo account due to legal or security obligations, you may include a built-in demo mode in lieu of a demo account with prior approval by Apple. Ensure the demo mode exhibits your app's full features and functionality. We will reject incomplete app bundles and binaries that crash or exhibit obvious technical problems." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 2.3.1: new features must be described specifically in the Notes for Review; hidden or undocumented features are grounds for rejection. — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Rejection precedent (Sept 2024, Apple Developer Forums, verbatim rejection text quoted by the developer): "Guideline 3.1.1 - Business - Payments - In-App Purchase. The app unlocks or enables additional functionality with mechanisms other than in-app purchase, which is not appropriate. Specifically, the app uses API keys to unlock or enable functionality. Next Steps: It would be appropriate to remove these features from the app and any other feature that unlocks or enables functionality with mechanisms other than the App Store. You may also consider making this unlocked content available to your users as in-app purchases." The app's model was "Pay once for the app. Insert your OpenAI/Claude key. Do stuff with AI." An App Review staff account replied "We're investigating currently. A representative from App Review will contact you"; no public outcome. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 763884](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/763884)
- Related precedent: a developer whose app unlocked via product key citing 3.1.3(b) reported the reviewer "didn't engage with that argument". — [Apple Developer Forums thread 713044](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/713044)
- Approval precedent (live listing): "Pal Chat - AI Chat Client" (Pulkit Agarwal, id6447545085) is on the US App Store; the listing says the app "necessitates an API Key to function", supports OpenAI/Anthropic/Groq/Perplexity/OpenRouter/DeepSeek/Mistral/custom endpoints, "NO data is collected", history on-device. — [apps.apple.com Pal Chat](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pal-chat-ai-chat-client/id6447545085); [origin forum post](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/739875)
- Approval precedent (live listing): "OpenCat - Chat with AI bot" (Early Moon LLC, id6445999201) offers "BYOK — use your own API Key" with 25+ providers including Gemini, alongside a cloud subscription, downloadable local models and Apple Foundation Models. — [apps.apple.com OpenCat](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/opencat-chat-with-ai-bot/id6445999201)
- 2025 US change: after the April 30, 2025 Epic v. Apple contempt order, Apple updated 3.1.1, 3.1.1(a), 3.1.3 and 3.1.3(a) on May 2, 2025 so that buttons/external links/calls to action are no longer prohibited on the US storefront and no entitlement is required; the change is US-only and does not permit alternative payment processing inside the app. — [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/news/developers-and-platforms-react-as-app-store-rules-change-in-the-u-s/); [mjtsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/05/02/app-review-guidelines-updated-for-epic-anti-steering); [FKKS](https://fkks.com/news/court-finds-apple-violated-order-resulting-in-key-changes-for-ios-external-purchase-methods)
- Google key-handling guidance (verbatim): "Never expose keys client-side in production"; "Do not hardcode API keys directly in web or mobile apps."; "Keys compiled in client-side code can be extracted by users."; "To secure client-side apps, run a backend proxy server to make the actual API calls." Key creation link: https://aistudio.google.com/apikey ("Create or view a Gemini API Key"). — [Google: Using Gemini API keys](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/api-key)
- OpenAI's stance on BYOK (for comparison only; not Google): OpenAI community staff said users "are not permitted to share their API keys with others, including via bring-your-own-key applications", while another thread says BYOK is "not strictly prohibited" but discouraged. — [OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/openais-bring-your-own-key-policy/14538); [OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/bring-your-own-key-policy/446168)

### Inferences
- The 2024 rejection and the live Pal Chat/OpenCat listings are consistent if you read reviewers as objecting to a key that gates the app's main value behind something bought outside Apple. Pal Chat and OpenCat are free downloads (OpenCat also sells an Apple IAP cloud tier); the rejected app and Mnemo are paid-up-front plus key. Mnemo's exposure is therefore higher than Pal Chat's. Mitigations that follow directly from the text: (a) make the paid download deliver real value without any key (manual flashcards, spaced repetition, imported decks, podcast playback of existing audio), so the key "connects a service" rather than "unlocks the app"; (b) never phrase the key screen as "unlock"/"activate"/"premium"; (c) put a working Gemini key in App Review notes and say so under 2.1(a) so the reviewer never sees a dead end; (d) consider a small optional IAP (e.g. "Mnemo Cloud" sync tier) so 3.1.3(b)'s "also available as in-app purchases" argument exists if challenged.
- Google's "do not hardcode API keys in mobile apps" is about the developer's own key being extractable; a user-entered key stored in Keychain/Preferences on the user's device is a different threat model, but reviewers and Google could still read the sentence against a client-side-calling app. A thin Vercel proxy that forwards the user's key per-request without storing it would satisfy the letter of Google's guidance at the cost of your "data never touches our server in plaintext" story; note the trade-off.
- Linking to aistudio.google.com from inside the app is not a link to "purchase digital content or services" (keys are free), so 3.1.1(a)/3.1.3 anti-steering does not apply by its terms; outside the US, keep the link informational ("Get a free key from Google") rather than a call to buy anything.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 written rejection specifically about BYOK was found; the only quoted rejection is Sept 2024 and its resolution is unknown. Reddit (r/iOSProgramming) is blocked for this crawler, so that corpus was not checked.
- Apple has no published position on whether a user-supplied third-party API key is a "mechanism to unlock functionality"; the rule is applied case by case.

## 4. Google Gemini API terms: BYOK, free tier, minors, consumer use

### Takeaway
Google's current Additional Terms (effective March 23, 2026) contain three clauses that cut directly against this product: users must be 18+, API Clients may not be "directed towards or ... likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18", and the API "is for developers building with Google AI models for professional or business purposes, not for consumer use". Free-tier (Unpaid Services) prompts and outputs are used to train/improve Google products and may be read by human reviewers; Google says "Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information to the Unpaid Services". In the EEA/UK/Switzerland only Paid Services may be used when making an app available to users. Nothing in the terms expressly permits or forbids an end user supplying their own key to a third-party app.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed, Age Requirements (verbatim): "You must be 18 years of age or older to use the APIs. You also will not use the Services as part of a website, application, or other service (collectively, 'API Clients') that is directed towards or is likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18." — [Gemini API Additional Terms of Service](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)
- Confirmed, Use Restrictions (verbatim): "Use of Google AI Studio and Gemini API is for developers building with Google AI models for professional or business purposes, not for consumer use. You may only access the Services (or make API Clients available to users) within an available region. You may use only Paid Services when making API Clients available to users in the European Economic Area, Switzerland, or the United Kingdom. You may not use the Services to develop models that compete with the Services..." — [Gemini API Additional Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)
- Confirmed, Unpaid Services (verbatim): "Any Services that are offered free of charge like direct interactions with Google AI Studio or unpaid quota in Gemini API are unpaid Services (the 'Unpaid Services')." ... "When you use Unpaid Services, including, for example, Google AI Studio and the unpaid quota on Gemini API, Google uses the content you submit to the Services and any generated responses to provide, improve, and develop Google products and services and machine learning technologies, including Google's enterprise features, products, and services, consistent with our Privacy Policy. To help with quality and improve our products, human reviewers may read, annotate, and process your API input and output. Google takes steps to protect your privacy as part of this process. This includes disconnecting this data from your Google Account, API key, and Cloud project before reviewers see or annotate it. Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information to the Unpaid Services." — [Gemini API Additional Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)
- Confirmed, Paid Services: Google "doesn't use your prompts (including associated system instructions, cached content, and files...)" to improve products; "Google logs prompts and responses for a limited period of time" solely for detecting/preventing Prohibited Use Policy violations. — [Gemini API Additional Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)
- Confirmed, Use of Generated Content (verbatim): "As required by the API Terms, you'll comply with applicable law in using generated content, which may require the provision of attribution to your users when returned as part of an API call. Use discretion before relying on generated content, including code. You're responsible for your use of generated content, and for the use of that content by anyone you share it with." — [Gemini API Additional Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)
- Confirmed: users "must comply with our Prohibited Use Policy". — [Gemini API Additional Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)
- Confirmed, pricing page: Free tier is "For developers and small projects getting started with the Gemini API"; Paid tier is "For production applications that require higher volumes and advanced features"; free tier rows read "Content used to improve our products", paid rows "Content not used to improve our products". Free tier exists for Gemini 3.8 Flash, 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, 3.1 Flash-Lite, 3 Flash Preview, 2.5 Pro/Flash/Flash-Lite, several TTS/Live/Transcribe models, Gemini Embedding 2 and Gemma 4; no free tier for Gemini 4 Argon, 3.1 Pro Preview, image models (Nano Banana / Gemini Omni Flash), Veo 3.1, Lyria. "Rate limits are subject to change." — [Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)
- Confirmed, regions page: Gemini API and AI Studio are available in the US, UK, Germany, Canada, Australia, India, Switzerland and most EEA states; the page carries the notice "Age requirements: You do not meet the minimum age requirement (18+) to access this service." — [Available regions](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/available-regions)
- Confirmed, Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy (last modified December 17, 2024): prohibits "dangerous or illegal activities", child sexual abuse content, "Circumvention of abuse protections or safety filters -- for example, manipulating the model to contravene our policies", sexually explicit content; allows exceptions "based on educational, documentary, scientific, or artistic considerations". It contains nothing about academic dishonesty, minors generally, or reselling/redistributing access. — [Google Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy](https://policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai/use-policy)

### Inferences
- The under-18 clause binds whoever accepts the terms. In a BYOK model each student accepts Google's terms when creating a key, so a 16-year-old creating a key is in breach of Google's terms, not yours; but the second sentence ("You also will not use the Services as part of a ... application ... likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18") binds the developer of the API Client too, and a study app marketed to high-schoolers is plainly "likely to be accessed" by minors. Apple's 5.2.2 ("ensure that you are specifically permitted to do so under the service's terms of use. Authorization must be provided upon request") turns this Google-terms problem into a potential Apple rejection. Realistic paths: (i) age-gate to 18+ (set App Store rating to 18+ via the "higher minimum age" option, state it in-app, use Declared Age Range API), which shrinks the student market; (ii) move AI calls behind your own paid Google Cloud/Vertex AI account under Google Cloud terms (different terms, not researched here) and sell via IAP, which abandons BYOK; (iii) ship BYOK as-is and accept the terms risk, which is a business decision to flag.
- "Not for consumer use" is in tension with any consumer BYOK app, and Google has not enforced it against Pal Chat/OpenCat-type apps as far as public record shows; still, it is the clause Google could cite if it ever wanted to shut down client-side consumer use of AI Studio keys.
- Because free-tier prompts are training data and human-reviewable, the in-app key screen should tell users that "a free Google AI Studio key means Google may use your uploaded notes to improve its products; enable billing in Google Cloud to opt out", and the privacy policy must say the same (Apple 5.1.1(i), 5.1.2(i)). Users in the EEA/UK/CH must be on a paid key per Google's terms.

### Gaps
- Google's terms are silent on third-party BYOK apps; no Google statement for or against was found.
- Free-tier rate limits were not pulled from the rate-limits page (pricing page cites only "generous limits", 5,000 search-grounding requests/month for Gemini 3+, and 500-1,500 RPD for 2.5-era tool use).
- Google Cloud / Vertex AI terms (the alternative to AI Studio keys) were out of scope.

## 5. Age rating, Kids Category, and 2025-2026 age-assurance laws

### Takeaway
Mnemo should not go in the Kids Category (1.3/5.1.4 forbid third-party data flows and "For Kids" wording); it should be rated under the 2025 questionnaire, where Apple explicitly says AI assistants/chatbot functionality must be considered, and should probably set 13+ (Thea is 13+) or, if following Google's 18+ terms, use Apple's "set a higher age rating" option to 18+. Texas's App Store Accountability Act is in force for new Texas Apple Accounts since June 4, 2026 (after a stayed injunction), Utah since May 6, 2026, Louisiana since July 1, 2026; all apply to new accounts and push developers toward the Declared Age Range API and Significant Change API.

## 6. Privacy: nutrition label, privacy policy, 5.1.2(i) third-party AI consent, Privacy Manifest

### Takeaway
Apple's definition of "collect" is transmission off device in a way that lets you or a third party access it "for a period longer than what is necessary to service the transmitted request in real time"; ciphertext-only sync your server cannot read is still "transmitted and stored", and Apple does not carve out end-to-end encryption, so the conservative label is "User Content (Other User Content) — not linked to you — app functionality". Data the user sends to Gemini is a disclosure to "third-party AI" under 5.1.2(i) (amended Nov 13, 2025) and requires explicit, in-app, prior permission. A Capacitor 6+/7+ app must ship PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy with required-reason API entries (UserDefaults CA92.1 at minimum for Preferences).

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed rule, 5.1.1(i) (verbatim): "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner. The privacy policy must clearly and explicitly: Identify what data, if any, the app/service collects, how it collects that data, and all uses of that data. Confirm that any third party with whom an app shares user data (in compliance with these Guidelines)—such as analytics tools, advertising networks and third-party SDKs, as well as any parent, subsidiary or other related entities that will have access to user data—will provide the same or equal protection of user data as stated in the app's privacy policy and required by these Guidelines. Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion of the user's data." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 5.1.1(ii) (verbatim excerpt): "Apps that collect user or usage data must secure user consent for the collection, even if such data is considered to be anonymous... Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data. Apps must also provide the customer with an easily accessible and understandable way to withdraw consent. Ensure your purpose strings clearly and completely describe your use of the data." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 5.1.2(i) (verbatim excerpt): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); the "third-party AI" wording was added in the November 13, 2025 guideline revision — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/apple-tightens-app-store-age-controls-and-data-sharing-disclosure/); [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/apple-app-store-ai-data-disclosure-guidelines); [stora.sh implementation guide](https://stora.sh/blog/2026-05-06-apple-ai-consent-rule-5-1-2-i-implementation-guide)
- Confirmed rule, 5.1.1(iii): request only data relevant to core functionality; prefer the out-of-process picker/share sheet over full Photos access. — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed, App Privacy Details definition (verbatim): "'Collect' refers to transmitting data off the device in a way that allows you and/or your third-party partners to access it for a period longer than what is necessary to service the transmitted request in real time." and "'Collect' refers to transmitting data off the device and storing it in a readable form for longer than the time it takes you and/or your third-party partners to service the request." — [Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- Confirmed (verbatim): "Data that is processed only on device is not 'collected' and does not need to be disclosed in your answers." and "if an authentication token or IP address is sent on a server call and not retained, or if data is sent to your servers then immediately discarded after servicing the request, you do not need to disclose this". — [Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- Confirmed (verbatim): free-form fields -> "Mark 'Other User Content' to represent generic free form text fields and 'Audio Data' for voice recordings... if you have a feature that enables users to upload a particular media type, such as photos or videos, then you'll need to disclose the specific type of data." — [Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- Confirmed: "Not linked" requires stripping direct identifiers and not attempting re-linkage; "'Personal Information' and 'Personal Data', as defined under relevant privacy laws, are considered linked to the user." The page does not mention end-to-end encryption and does not say encryption exempts data from disclosure. — [Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- Confirmed: "You need to identify all of the data you or your third-party partners collect"; "Third-party partners" = "analytics tools, advertising networks, third-party SDKs, or other external vendors whose code you've added to your app." — [Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- Confirmed, Capacitor docs: Apple began notifying about missing approved reasons March 13, 2024 and required them from May 1, 2024; use Capacitor 6.0.0+ (or 5.7.4+); plugins such as @capacitor/filesystem and @capacitor/preferences "may necessitate a Privacy Manifest File"; sample PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy declares NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryUserDefaults with reason CA92.1. — [Capacitor iOS Privacy Manifest](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/ios/privacy-manifest)
- Precedent for how a pure-BYOK client labels itself: Pal Chat's listing states "NO data is collected" with history on-device. — [apps.apple.com Pal Chat](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pal-chat-ai-chat-client/id6447545085)
- Comparison: Thea declares Location, Contact info, User content (photos/videos), Identifiers, Usage, Diagnostics all linked to user. — [apps.apple.com Thea](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/thea-study-smart/id6742800289)

### Inferences
- Gemini calls from the device: Google is not "code you've added to your app" in the SDK sense if you call the REST API directly, and Google's paid-tier logs are "for a limited period" for abuse detection; under a strict reading the user's uploads to Google are not Mnemo's "collection", but 5.1.2(i) is independent of the label and requires a consent screen before the first upload to Gemini. The free tier, where Google trains on the content, makes the disclosure materially important; a one-time consent screen with a link to Google's terms, plus a settings toggle, satisfies the text.
- Sync: the Vercel endpoint stores ciphertext for longer than the request, so disclose "Other User Content" (and "User ID"/sync code identifier if one exists) as collected, "not linked to you", purpose "App Functionality", and explain in the privacy policy that the server holds only AES-256-GCM ciphertext it cannot read. Declaring "Data Not Collected" would be defensible only if nothing is retained server-side.
- Privacy policy must also state retention/deletion for sync blobs and how a user deletes them (5.1.1(i), and 5.1.1(v) account-deletion logic by analogy).
- Required-reason APIs: WKWebView itself does not need entries, but Capacitor Preferences (UserDefaults, CA92.1) and Filesystem (file timestamps, C617.1) do; add them to the Xcode target.

### Gaps
- Apple has no written guidance on how to label end-to-end-encrypted blobs; the "not linked / Other User Content" mapping above is an inference from the definitions, not an Apple statement.
- Apple's own Nov 13, 2025 developer-news post text for the 5.1.2(i) change was not fetched directly; the current guideline text (quoted) is primary.

## 7. Guideline 5.1.1(v): no-account apps and "sync code" identity

### Takeaway
No account is the preferred state under 5.1.1(v); a sync code is not an account. The only requirements that bite are that the app must function without a login and must not demand personal information that is not core; if a sync code is user-generated and deletable, there is no account-deletion obligation, but offering "delete my synced data" is cheap insurance.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed rule, 5.1.1(v) (verbatim): "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login. If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app. Apps may not require users to enter personal information to function, except when directly relevant to the core functionality of the app or required by law. If your core app functionality is not related to a specific social network..., you must provide access without a login or via another mechanism... An app may not store credentials or tokens to social networks off of the device..." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 5.1.1(ii): "Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- A pasted Gemini key is "personal information" in the loose sense (it is a credential); requiring it for AI features is defensible as "directly relevant to the core functionality", which is exactly why the non-AI features should remain usable without it (ties back to section 3).
- The social-network token sentence (no off-device storage of tokens) is about social logins, but it signals Apple's attitude: never send the user's Gemini key to the Vercel backend for storage.

### Gaps
- No precedent of a reviewer objecting to a sync-code/passphrase model was found.

## 8. Guidelines 2.5.x for the WebView; IndexedDB, dictation, background audio

### Takeaway
A Capacitor app that bundles its JS at build time satisfies 2.5.2 (no downloaded code that changes features); hot-updating the web bundle (Capgo-style live updates) is the thing to avoid or do conservatively. iOS can evict WKWebView IndexedDB under storage pressure, so Capacitor recommends native Preferences/SQLite for data that must survive; keyboard dictation works in web inputs without any permission, while SFSpeechRecognizer via a plugin needs NSSpeechRecognitionUsageDescription and NSMicrophoneUsageDescription; HTML5 audio in WKWebView in the background has a long record of unreliability even with UIBackgroundModes audio.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed rule, 2.5.2 (verbatim): "Apps should be self-contained in their bundles, and may not read or write data outside the designated container area, nor may they download, install, or execute code which introduces or changes features or functionality of the app, including other apps. Educational apps designed to teach, develop, or allow students to test executable code may, in limited circumstances, download code provided that such code is not used for other purposes." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 2.5.6: apps that browse the web must use WebKit and WebKit JavaScript (alternative engines only by entitlement in the EU and Japan). — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 4.7: separate rules apply to apps that host HTML5 mini-apps/chatbots/plug-ins inside themselves (content filtering, IAP, age restriction via 4.7.5); not applicable to an app whose own UI is HTML, but 4.7.5 shows Apple's expectation that "software that exceeds the app's age rating" be gated by "verified or declared age". — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed, Capacitor Storage guide: the OS reclaims local storage from web views when a device is low on space, "the same applies to IndexedDB at least on iOS"; the native Preferences API "avoids the eviction issues above, but is meant for small amounts of data"; SQLite is recommended for larger data. — [Capacitor Storage guide](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/guides/storage)
- Anecdote: a developer says `navigator.storage.persist()` is "not reliable" in WKWebView and cites WebKit's 7-day cap on script-writable storage. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 773434](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/773434); [gitea TASK_storage.md](https://gitea.anomalistdesign.com/trent_larson/crowd-funder-for-time-pwa/raw/branch/capacitor-local-save/TASK_storage.md)
- Confirmed (plugin README): @capacitor-community/speech-recognition requires NSSpeechRecognitionUsageDescription and NSMicrophoneUsageDescription in Info.plist; Capgo's fork defaults to SFSpeechRecognizer with opt-in SpeechAnalyzer on iOS 26+. — [capacitor-community/speech-recognition](https://github.com/capacitor-community/speech-recognition); [Cap-go/capacitor-speech-recognition](https://github.com/Cap-go/capacitor-speech-recognition)
- Anecdote: a Capacitor app with a duplicate NSMicrophoneUsageDescription key skipped the permission prompt until the duplicate was removed. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 785757](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/785757)
- Anecdotes on background audio: one developer found WKWebView background audio kept working after removing the UIBackgroundModes audio key (possibly a debugger artefact); another on iOS 17.2.1 reported playback failing in the background even with background modes set; iOS 13 and iOS 15 threads describe RBS assertion errors suspending WebKit media; an iOS 18.1 report says YouTube-in-WKWebView audio usually stops in the background; Capgo recommends UIBackgroundModes audio + AVAudioSession .playback and "native playback for music or podcast apps". — [Apple Developer Forums 713084](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/713084); [Apple Developer Forums 781787](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/781787); [Apple Developer Forums 121822](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/121822?page=3); [Capgo background audio](https://capgo.app/blog/how-to-play-audio-in-the-background-in-capacitor/)

### Inferences
- The podcast feature is the one place where the WebView-only approach is likely to fail a real-device test; plan to route audio through a native plugin (or at least verify on a physical device with the debugger detached) and declare UIBackgroundModes audio only if it is actually used, since an unused background mode is itself a classic rejection reason (2.5.4, not quoted here).
- If decks and AI outputs live only in IndexedDB, iOS eviction could silently wipe a user's library; mirror critical data to SQLite/Filesystem or rely on the encrypted sync as the durable copy and say so in onboarding.
- Keyboard dictation (the microphone key on the iOS keyboard) works in a WKWebView text input without any plist key and is the zero-permission path; use SFSpeechRecognizer only if you need streaming transcription, and then add both usage strings and the Speech capability.

### Gaps
- No Apple documentation on WKWebView background audio semantics was found; evidence is forum-only.
- Guideline 2.5.4 (background modes) was not quoted in this pass.

## 9. Export compliance for AES-256-GCM

### Takeaway
Apple's App Store Connect table says encryption "limited to that within the Apple operating system" needs no documentation; "an industry standard algorithm, not provided within the Apple operating system" needs only a French encryption declaration (and only for distribution in France); proprietary algorithms need CCATS. AES-256-GCM via WebCrypto in WKWebView is implemented by the OS's WebKit/CommonCrypto and is a standard algorithm, so the likely answers are ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption = NO (or YES + "standard algorithms" exemption) with no CCATS; Apple notes a year-end self-classification report to the US government "might" be required, which community sources say has not applied to mass-market standard-crypto software since March 2021.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed (verbatim): "Add the ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption key to your app's information property list with a Boolean value that indicates whether your app uses encryption. Set the value to NO if your app—including any third-party libraries it links against—doesn't use encryption, or if it only uses forms of encryption that are exempt from export compliance documentation requirements. Otherwise, set it to YES." — [Apple: Complying with Encryption Export Regulations](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/complying-with-encryption-export-regulations)
- Confirmed (verbatim): "Typically, the use of encryption that's built into the operating system—for example, when your app makes HTTPS connections using URLSession—is exempt from export documentation upload requirements, whereas the use of proprietary encryption is not." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/complying-with-encryption-export-regulations)
- Confirmed (verbatim): "If your app uses exempt forms of encryption, you might alternatively be required to submit a year-end self-classification report to the U.S. government. (If you use non-exempt encryption and provide documentation to Apple, the self-classification report isn't necessary.)" — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/complying-with-encryption-export-regulations)
- Confirmed, App Store Connect reference table (verbatim): "Your app uses encryption limited to that within the Apple operating system | No documentation required in App Store Connect." / "Your app uses an industry standard algorithm, not provided within the Apple operating system | Upload your French encryption declaration in App Store Connect." / "Your app uses proprietary encryption algorithms not accepted by international standard bodies (such as IEEE, IETF, or ITU) | Upload your: US Commodity Classification Automated Tracking System (CCATS); French encryption declaration in App Store Connect" with footnote "French encryption declaration form is only required if you're distributing your app on the App Store in France." — [App Store Connect Help: Export compliance documentation for encryption](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/export-compliance-documentation-for-encryption/); see also [Determine and upload app encryption documentation](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/determine-and-upload-app-encryption-documentation/)
- Community (Proxmox developers, Sept-Oct 2025): the annual BIS self-classification report requirement for mass-market software using standard algorithms was removed effective March 29, 2021, except for "non-standard" cryptography; a TLS+SHA-256 app was judged ECCN 5D992 mass market. — [pve-devel patch thread](https://lists.proxmox.com/pipermail/pve-devel/2025-October/075657.html); contradicted by an older Apple-forum post asserting HTTPS apps must file a year-end report — [Apple Developer Forums 684398](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/684398) (pre-2021 framing, less reliable)

### Inferences
- Choose one of two defensible positions and record it: (a) answer "No" to ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption on the basis that WebCrypto AES-GCM is encryption "within the Apple operating system" (WebKit is OS-provided); or (b) answer "Yes", then in App Store Connect select that the app uses only standard algorithms, which yields the "French declaration only if sold in France" outcome. Either way no CCATS. If the Capacitor project pulls in a JS crypto library (e.g. libsodium/tweetnacl) rather than WebCrypto, position (b) is the honest one.
- The 2021 BIS change means a self-classification report is very unlikely to be required for AES-GCM; this is community-sourced, so a one-line check against 15 CFR 740.17 or a compliance adviser is prudent before relying on it.

### Gaps
- The BIS primary text confirming the 2021 self-classification change was not fetched.
- Apple's France declaration form details were not fetched.

## 10. Practical submission checklist for a Capacitor-wrapped Vite app, and 2026 review timing

### Takeaway
Apple's official line is that 90% of submissions are reviewed within 24-48 hours (Apple stated "90% within 48 hours, average 1.5 days" in June 2026); third-party trackers in mid-2026 show roughly 9-14 hours waiting plus about 1.5 hours in review for iOS, with Mac taking longer. First submissions of a novel BYOK/AI app should expect "greater scrutiny" per Apple's own text. The checklist below is assembled from the guideline text quoted above plus Capacitor/vendor guidance.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed (verbatim, "After You Submit"): "App Review will examine your app as soon as we can. However, if your app is complex or presents new issues, it may require greater scrutiny and consideration... if your app is repeatedly rejected for the same guideline violation or you've attempted to manipulate the review process, review of your app will take longer to complete."

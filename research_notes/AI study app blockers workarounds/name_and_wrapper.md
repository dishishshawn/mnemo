# App name and native-wrapper route for shipping Mnemo (React + Vite PWA) on the iOS App Store

Research date: 2026-10-14. This extends sections 1 and 2 of `../AI study app competitors and pricing/app_store_rules.md` (which already covers the 4.2 and 2.3.7 guideline text, the Jan 2026 Capacitor limo-app rejection, and the first sighting of the Keiichirou Hayashi "Mnemo" app). Nothing from those sections is repeated except where a new fact changes the conclusion.

Headline: (1) The bare name "Mnemo" is unavailable: App Store Connect enforces global, case-insensitive name uniqueness, a live Education app already holds the exact name, a second "Mnemo: Flashcards & Vocabulary" app launched 2026-09-18 and was updated three days before this research, and Mnemo, Inc. (Merchantville, NJ) has a US trademark application for MNEMO in Classes 9 and 42 that reached Notice of Allowance in July 2026. A compound like "Mnemo Study" would probably clear the uniqueness check but lands in a crowd of ~20 live Mnemo-prefixed apps, six of them flashcard apps. Five invented alternatives tested clean on every storefront queried and in a US trademark search: Mnemonote, Memoglyph, Penmemo, Memorook, Recallery. (2) Capacitor remains the cheapest credible route; Tauri 2 mobile lacks an iOS SQL plugin and a share-sheet plugin and has no documented 4.2 approval; a hand-rolled SwiftUI/WKWebView shell (what PWABuilder generates) is explicitly labelled "Experimental" by its own maintainers. No 2025-2026 case of a wrapped app being rejected under 4.2 and then approved with Apple's text quoted exists in public; the best-documented recovery (Ionic, Dec 2020) passed on the third submission after removing external links and improving offline, not after adding plugins.

## Part 1, Q1. App Store Connect name rules: uniqueness, what is enforced, the error text, 30 characters, subtitle, 2.3.7

### Takeaway
Apple's official help text defines the Name as 2-30 characters, editable until first submission, and says nothing about uniqueness; the uniqueness rule is enforced by App Store Connect itself with the error "the app name you entered is already being used," and Apple's legal site offers an "App Name Dispute" form for trademark holders. Community evidence says the check is global across storefronts, case-insensitive, and includes unreleased/reserved apps; punctuation tricks have worked historically but Apple's product-page guidance says to avoid names "too similar to existing app names." The subtitle has no uniqueness rule, so "Mnemo" + subtitle is not a workaround for a taken name.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed rule (App Store Connect Help, "App information", fetched 2026-10-14): "The localized name of your app as it appears on App Store product pages and when users install your app. The name must be at least two characters and no more than 30 characters. You can edit it until you submit the app to App Review. Later, you can change the name when you create a new version or the status of the app version permits editing this property." Subtitle: "A summary of your app that appears under your app's name on your App Store product page. This can't be longer than 30 characters." The page says nothing about uniqueness or trademarks. — [Apple ASC Help: App information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-information)
- Confirmed guidance (Apple product-page marketing guide, fetched 2026-10-14): "Choose a simple, memorable name that is easy to spell and hints at what your app does. Be distinctive. Avoid names that use generic terms or are too similar to existing app names. An app name can be up to 30 characters long." Subtitle: "Consider using this, rather than your app's name, to explain the value of your app in greater detail... A subtitle can be up to 30 characters long." Keywords: "Do not use... Competing app names... Improper use of keywords is a common reason for App Store rejections." — [Apple: Creating your product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- Exact error text (developer quoting App Store Connect, July 2022): "English (U.S.) Name couldn't be saved because the app name you entered is already being used. If you have trademark rights to this name and would like it released for your use, submit a claim." The developer had renamed and deleted the blocking app in another account; two days later the name was still unavailable in the default locale while other localizations saved fine. No Apple staff reply. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 711244](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/711244)
- Anecdote (Apple Developer Forums, Nov 2017): the name may belong to "an app under development, temporarily removed from sale, or available only in another country's App Store"; developers can "reserve" a name by creating an unpublished app record; Apple "has previously allowed names that differ from an existing app by only one character, such as a space or punctuation mark," with results varying. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 91335](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/91335)
- Anecdote (Aug 2022): a developer whose name was being used by another developer's near-duplicate reported Apple "refuses to even acknowledge this issue"; community advice was to use Contact Us > "Notify a problem" > "App name", and that "Apple makes no promises about app name uniqueness" beyond exact matches. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 711554](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/711554)
- Secondary summary (PTKD Journal, undated, footer 2026): "the app name is unique across the entire store"; "A name that differs from an existing one only by capitalization is treated as the same name"; subtitles "have no uniqueness requirement and can repeat across apps"; the on-device home-screen label (CFBundleDisplayName) is set separately and need not be unique; names reserved but never submitted are released after "a defined period" (unspecified). — [PTKD: Can two apps have the same name](https://ptkd.com/journal/app-store-same-name-availability)
- Historical anecdote (2011): developers appended a period, plus sign or ™ to squat or bypass taken names. — [Ben Guild, Attack of the App Store name squatters](https://benguild.com/2011/09/19/attack-of-the-itunes-app-store-name-squatters/)
- Confirmed process (Apple Legal, fetched 2026-10-14), App Name Dispute: "If you believe an App is preventing you from using your trademark as an app name on the App Store, you can submit your claim here... After you provide us with the required information, we will identify the app(s) blocking the name you wish to use" and Apple then asks the other provider to resolve it with you directly. The page refers to "your trademark" without stating that a registration is required. Separate note: if the blocking app is in your own or an associated account, "please contact the App Store Connect team for further handling." — [Apple: App Store Content Dispute / App Name Dispute](https://www.apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/dispute-forms/app-store)
- Law-firm commentary on the dispute route: a registered mark "is not a strict requirement" but makes ownership easier to establish; Apple typically only forwards the claim and asks the parties to work it out; estimated turnaround "a week or two." — [Harper James: Protecting your app's trade mark](https://harperjames.co.uk/article/protecting-your-apps-trade-mark/); [BCF: Infringement of your rights by an app](https://www.bcf.ca/en/thought-leadership/2019/infringement-of-your-rights-by-an-app-let-apple-help-you)
- 2.3.7 text (verbatim) is in the prior notes, section 2; key clause for compound names: "Choose a unique app name, assign keywords that accurately describe your app, and don't try to pack any of your metadata with trademarked terms, popular app names, pricing information, or other irrelevant phrases just to game the system. App names must be limited to 30 characters." — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- Because "Mnemo" is held by a live app in the same category, App Store Connect will refuse the bare name in every locale, and the subtitle field cannot rescue it. A compound ("Mnemo Study", "Mnemo AI", "Mnemo: Study Kits") is a different string and should pass the automated check (no exact match found on any storefront queried, see Q2), but Apple's own guidance to avoid names "too similar to existing app names," plus 4.3(b) spam and 5.2.1 copycat latitude, means a reviewer could still push back given six existing Mnemo-prefixed flashcard apps.
- The App Name Dispute form is useless to this developer: it is for trademark holders whose name is blocked, and here the blocking app's owner has three years of priority with no registration by anyone except Mnemo, Inc.'s pending application for unrelated software.
- Punctuation or case variants ("MNEMO", "Mnemo.") are the highest-risk option: historically they sometimes pass the field validator, then collide with 2.3.7/4.1 at review.

### Gaps
- Apple publishes no document stating the uniqueness algorithm (exact vs. normalized), the name-reservation expiry period, or whether the check spans all storefronts; all of that is forum/blog evidence. The only way to confirm a specific name is to create the app record in App Store Connect (free once enrolled) and watch for the error.

### Inferences
- Likelihood-of-confusion analysis is about marks plus related goods. MNEMO (image software, Class 9/42) vs. a study app in Class 9/42 is the same mark in the same classes; an examiner would very likely cite Mnemo, Inc.'s allowed application against any later "MNEMO"-formative filing for software, and once it registers (after the Statement of Use) Mnemo, Inc. would have standing to file an App Name Dispute or Content Dispute against a "Mnemo"-named app. The practical risk today is low (a small NJ company, unrelated product) but it removes "Mnemo" as a brand the developer could ever own.
- The Spanish MNEMO firm is a B2B security vendor in a different field with no US software registration found; objection risk is low. The Maruman MNEMOSYNE notebook mark is Class 16 (paper goods), not software, but Saluca's March 2026 Class 9/42 MNEMOSYNE filings are a direct software conflict for anyone wanting that name.

### Gaps
- No USPTO TESS/TSDR query could be run directly (API key required; JS front-end). GleanMark and Trademarkia are aggregators of USPTO data; the Mnemo, Inc. record should be confirmed at tsdr.uspto.gov for serial 99547128 before relying on it. No WIPO Global Brand Database or EUIPO eSearch query could be executed (JS-only interfaces), so EU/international coverage is "not found in web search," not cleared.

### Takeaway

## Part 2, Q3. Staying a PWA on iOS 26 and selling a web license instead of using the App Store

### Takeaway
iOS 26 removed every installability requirement (any site added to the Home Screen opens as a web app by default) and installed web apps get Web Push (iOS 16.4+), Declarative Web Push (18.4+), the Badging API, up to 60% of disk per origin, and exemption from ITP's 7-day storage cap because they keep their own usage counter. What they still cannot do: reliable background/lock-screen audio (2025 WebKit bugs show Web Audio in home-screen apps working only on first launch on iOS 26), Background Sync, programmatic install prompts, and silent push. Selling a $2.99 web license is mechanically easy but fee-heavy: Paddle and Lemon Squeezy take 5% + $0.50 (22% of $2.99), Stripe Managed Payments takes 6.4% + $0.30 (16%), plain Stripe 2.9% + $0.30 (13%) but leaves sales tax/VAT to you, versus Apple's 15% Small Business rate plus $99/yr.

### Cited Findings
- WebKit (Sept 15, 2025, Safari 26.0): "By default, every website added to the Home Screen opens as a web app... If the user prefers to add a bookmark for their browser, they can disable 'Open as Web App'... Giving users a web app experience simply no longer requires a manifest file. Simply put, there are now zero requirements for 'installability' in Safari." Also in Safari 26: WebGPU, Digital Credentials API, "support for the File System WritableStream API." — [WebKit features in Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)
- WebKit (Feb 16, 2023, iOS 16.4): "A web app that has been added to the Home Screen can request permission to receive push notifications" in response to direct user interaction; "Home Screen web apps on iOS and iPadOS 16.4 now support the Badging API"; notifications integrate with Focus. — [WebKit: Web Push for web apps on iOS and iPadOS](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)
- Declarative Web Push (no service worker needed) arrived for Home Screen web apps in iOS/iPadOS 18.4. — [WebKit: Meet Declarative Web Push](https://webkit.org/blog/16535/meet-declarative-web-push/)
- Storage (Safari 17 / iOS 17, Aug 10, 2023): "For a browser app, the origin quota is up to 60% of the total disk space"; overall "up to 80%"; a Home Screen web app "has the same origin quota and overall quota as when it is opened in a browser app"; eviction is LRU per origin, sparing origins with an active page or in persistent mode; WebKit grants `navigator.storage.persist()` using heuristics "such as whether the site is opened as a Home Screen Web App." — [WebKit: Updates to storage policy](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)
- 7-day cap (Mar 24, 2020): ITP deletes "all of a website's script-writable storage after seven days of Safari use without user interaction on the site" (IndexedDB, LocalStorage, SessionStorage, service worker registrations and cache), but "Web applications added to the home screen are not part of Safari and thus have their own counter of days of use. Their days of use will match actual use of the web application which resets the timer. We do not expect the first-party in such a web application to have its website data deleted." — [WebKit: Full third-party cookie blocking and more](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/)
- Background/lock-screen audio in home-screen web apps: Apple forum reply (2024) "On iOS, Apple has disabled background audio for PWA's... you can push it out as an actual app and it will work"; a developer reported lock-screen controls stop responding after ~30 s paused; WebKit bug 291892 (July 2025) `<audio>` plays on first launch of a home-screen app but fails after reopening; WebKit bug 295518 comment (Sept 2025): on iOS 26 "Any webpage added to the home screen as a PWA will only work with Web Audio on the first run." No fix confirmed. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 762582](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/762582); [WebKit bug 291892](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=291892); [WebKit bug 295518](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=295518)
- Still unsupported on iOS web apps: Background Sync, Web Bluetooth, Web NFC, WebUSB (declined by Apple in 2020); no `beforeinstallprompt`; push requires `userVisibleOnly: true` (no silent push); install is manual via the share sheet. — [Synthetixis: PWAs on iPhone in 2026](https://synthetixis.com/writing/pwa-ios-2026)
- Install friction: a developer reports "you now need at least five taps to add a PWA to your home screen" on iOS 26. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 801602](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/801602)
- Paddle pricing (fetched 2026-10-14): "5% + 50¢ per Checkout transaction" pay-as-you-go, "No monthly fees, migration fees, or hidden extras"; Paddle is merchant of record and files sales tax/VAT; FAQ says products under $10 "may get bespoke pricing" on request. — [Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)
- Lemon Squeezy pricing (fetched 2026-10-14): "5% + 50¢ per transaction"; non-US transactions "may encounter small additional fees"; payouts twice a month; footer "Sold through Link, LLC f/k/a Lemon Squeezy LLC" with Stripe badge. Its Jan 28, 2026 post says it is "building on Stripe's merchant of record product, Stripe Managed Payments," access "currently limited" with a waitlist, "Our goal is to provide Lemon Squeezy users an easy way to migrate to Stripe Managed Payments"; Managed Payments supports merchants in 35+ countries. — [Lemon Squeezy pricing](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/pricing); [Lemon Squeezy: 2026 Update](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/blog/2026-update)
- Stripe pricing (fetched 2026-10-14): "2.9% + 30¢ per successful transaction for domestic cards," "+1.5% for international cards," "+1% if currency conversion is required," and "3.5% per successful Managed Payments transaction in addition to Payments fees." — [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)
- Stripe Managed Payments (docs, fetched 2026-10-14): Stripe is merchant of record, handles "sales tax, VAT, and GST compliance in more than 80 countries," fraud, disputes and "transaction-level customer support"; works with Checkout and Payment Links only (not Elements); subscriptions via Billing; supported business locations include US, CA, GB, most of the EU, AU, HK, JP, SG; eligible products include "Software," "Online courses and training," with tax codes such as "Downloadable Software - non-recreational - personal use (educational...)" and "Software as a service - electronic download - personal use"; access is "based on an eligibility review"; a "mobile app payments" guide exists. — [Stripe docs: Managed Payments](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments); [Managed Payments eligibility](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/eligibility)
- Apple Small Business Program: "a reduced commission rate of 15% on paid apps and Apple In-App Purchases" for developers with up to $1M prior-year proceeds and new developers; requires accepting the Paid Apps agreement; EU alternative-terms developers get 10%. — [Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)

### Inferences
- Fee math on a single $2.99 sale (US domestic card): Paddle / Lemon Squeezy $0.65 (21.7%) net $2.34; Stripe Managed Payments $0.49 (16.4%) net $2.50; plain Stripe $0.39 (12.9%) net $2.60 but you are the merchant of record for sales tax, VAT and chargebacks; Apple SBP $0.45 (15%) net $2.54 with tax handled, minus $99/yr amortised. At $2.99 the merchant-of-record options cost 1.5-7 points more than Apple; the web route only wins on fees if volume is tiny (under ~33 sales/yr the $99 dominates) or the price is higher. Paddle's "under $10, ask for bespoke pricing" note is worth one email.
- For the product itself, the PWA path now loses only three things that matter to a study app: lock-screen podcast playback (broken/unreliable on iOS 26), discoverability (no store listing, five-tap install), and credibility with students who expect an App Store receipt. Storage eviction is a non-issue for an installed web app that is used regularly, and push/badge work. A reasonable hedge is to keep the PWA as the free tier and treat the App Store build as the paid, "native touches" tier.
- Lemon Squeezy is in wind-down toward Stripe Managed Payments; starting a new store there in late 2026 is choosing a migration. Paddle or Stripe Managed Payments (if the eligibility review passes; "educational downloadable software" is an eligible tax code) are the two stable merchant-of-record options for a solo developer.

### Gaps
- No Apple primary source on iOS 26 background audio for home-screen web apps; the evidence is two open WebKit bugs and a forum reply. Share Target and Screen Wake Lock status on iOS 26 were not confirmed from primary sources. Lemon Squeezy's international surcharge amounts and Stripe Managed Payments' current rollout status (public vs. invite) were not confirmed from primary pages.

## Part 2, Q4. Apple Developer Program: cost, individual enrollment, D-U-N-S, tax/banking, time to first sale

### Takeaway
Individuals pay $99/yr, need a two-factor Apple Account, legal name (shown as seller) and no D-U-N-S; Apple's only official timing statement is to contact support if no confirmation arrives within 24 hours of purchase, while forum reports range from hours to five days (and weeks in edge cases). To sell a paid app the Account Holder must accept the Paid Apps Agreement, submit a W-9 (US) and banking details; the agreement is "not in effect" until status is Active, and the banking "Processing" step is reported to take from two days to a month. Realistic enrollment-to-first-sale: about one to three weeks if the W-9 and bank details are entered on day one, with App Review (typically 24-48 h) overlapping.

### Cited Findings
- Enrollment page (fetched 2026-10-14): individuals and sole proprietors can enroll; "99 USD per membership year"; legal age of majority; Apple Account with two-factor; use your legal name because "Your name is shown as the seller name on the App Store"; P.O. boxes not accepted; D-U-N-S Number is listed only under organization requirements. — [Apple Developer Program: Enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- Enrollment support (fetched 2026-10-14): "If you haven't received a membership confirmation within 24 hours of purchase, contact Apple Developer Support and include your Enrollment ID"; individuals may be asked for a government-issued photo ID if paying with someone else's card; individuals can enroll on the web or in the Apple Developer app (India app-only; app membership is an auto-renewing subscription). — [Apple Developer Support: Enrollment](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/)
- Forum/guide timing: individual approvals "often within a day, sometimes within hours" (vendor guide); one individual reported membership not active five days after paying (Apple forum). — [Adalo: Apple Developer Program guide](https://studio.adalo.com/blog/apple-developer-program-guide); [Apple Developer Forums: Apple Developer Program topic](https://developer.apple.com/forums/topics/developer-tools-and-services/apple-developer-program?page=2)
- Paid Apps Agreement (ASC Help): must be signed by the Account Holder under Business > Agreements > "View and Agree to Terms"; "The agreement must be active before you can submit or update paid apps and In-App Purchases"; must be re-accepted on renewal and when new versions are issued (you cannot create a new app or IAP until accepted). — [ASC Help: Sign and update agreements](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-agreements/sign-and-update-agreements)
- Agreement statuses (ASC Help, verbatim): New "The agreement hasn't been signed"; Pending User Info "You've signed an agreement, but it's not in effect because you haven't finished providing required information"; Processing "You've provided all the information and we're reviewing your agreement"; Active "The agreement is in effect"; Active (Pending User) "in effect, but you haven't finished providing required information"; Disabled "your app is removed from the App Store until you provide the required information." — [ASC Help: View agreements status](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-agreements/view-agreements-status)
- Tax forms (ASC Help): "All developers must complete a US tax form to comply with the Paid Apps Agreement... If you're based in the United States, you're prompted to complete a W-9 form" using SSN (individuals) or EIN; "In order to submit tax forms, you first need to sign the Paid Apps Agreement"; "Once you submit this information, you won't be able to make any changes in App Store Connect"; role required Account Holder/Admin/Finance; no processing time stated. — [ASC Help: Tax forms overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/provide-tax-information/tax-forms-overview)
- Banking/processing anecdotes: bank account stuck in "Processing" for several weeks; replies in a 2019 thread report two days to one month; a 2025-2026 thread reports apps "stuck in Waiting for Review for 23 days due to pending agreements"; another reports Paid Apps "Pending User Info" while bank/tax show Active (a blank tax-form "Nickname" field was one suspected cause). — [Apple Developer Forums thread 115049](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/115049); [thread 817371](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/817371); [thread 810927](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/810927); [thread 810848](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/810848)
- Fee waivers apply only to nonprofits, accredited educational institutions and government entities distributing free apps. — [Apple Developer Program: Enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- Small Business Program proceeds adjustment "take[s] effect 15 days after the end of the fiscal month in which enrollment is approved"; enroll immediately after the Paid Apps agreement is accepted. — [Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)

### Inferences
- Sequence that minimises wall-clock time: enroll (day 0) > on activation, create the app record to lock the name and accept Paid Apps + W-9 + bank the same day > enroll in SBP > upload a TestFlight build while banking processes > submit for review once Agreements show Active. Expect 1-3 weeks to first sale; the agreements step, not App Review, is the long pole and is the one thing to start before the build is finished.
- Review timing for the build itself is covered in the prior notes, section 10.

### Gaps
- Apple publishes no SLA for individual enrollment verification or for banking/tax "Processing"; every duration above is a forum anecdote.

## Part 2, Q5. 2026 changes to Apple's web-app/PWA and EU rules that matter here

### Takeaway
Nothing in 2026 restricts PWAs further; the 2024 EU home-screen web-app removal was reversed on March 1, 2024 and Apple's current EU page no longer mentions it. The relevant 2026 changes are commercial: from October 1, 2026 EU apps are under unified terms with a 5% Core Technology Commission replacing the Core Technology Fee, alternative payment methods allowed alongside IAP in EU storefront apps, no EU legal-entity requirement for Web Distribution, and an updated install flow for alternatively distributed apps in iOS 27.2. On the web side, Safari 26 (Sept 2025) made every site installable and added WebGPU, Digital Credentials and File System WritableStream; iOS 26 web-audio regressions in home-screen apps are the notable negative.

### Cited Findings
- Apple EU DMA page (fetched 2026-10-14; references DPLA "updated August 18, 2026"): "Unified business terms (effective October 1, 2026)"; "The Core Technology Fee is replaced by the Core Technology Commission (5% on digital transactions in apps distributed outside the App Store)"; Initial Acquisition Fee and Store Services Fee eliminated; EU storefront apps "can offer alternative payment methods alongside Apple In-App Purchase" (must keep options for 12 months); under-13 users need a parental gate for alternative-payment purchases; "Companies no longer need a legal entity or EU establishment to operate an alternative app marketplace or use Web Distribution"; the Alternative Terms Addendum is superseded by Attachment 14 of the DPLA as of Oct 1, 2026; iOS/iPadOS 27.2 brings "an updated installation experience for alternative app marketplaces and apps distributed via a developer's website" and 90-day use outside the EU. Alternative browser engines remain available to entitled EU browser developers. The page does not mention Home Screen web apps. — [Apple: DMA and apps in the EU](https://developer.apple.com/support/dma-and-apps-in-the-eu/)
- 2024 saga: Apple removed Home Screen web apps for EU users in iOS 17.4 beta ("an entirely new integration architecture... not practical"), the EU opened an inquiry, and Apple reversed on March 1, 2024. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2666084); [PushAlert: Apple reverses decision](https://pushalert.co/blog/?p=2608); [Synthetixis](https://synthetixis.com/writing/pwa-ios-2026)
- Open Web Advocacy (2025-2026) argues Apple's EU browser-engine terms make alternative engines "financially unviable," so WebKit remains the only engine in practice. — [Thurrott: OWA says Apple making alternative engines unviable](https://www.thurrott.com/?p=323343)
- Apple delayed some iOS 26 features in the EU over DMA concerns and says it is "actively working on solutions." — [Thurrott: Apple to delay some iOS 26 features in the EU](https://www.thurrott.com/mobile/322784/apple-to-delay-some-ios-26-features-in-the-eu-over-dma-compliance-concerns)
- Safari 26 web-app and platform changes: see Q3 findings. — [WebKit features in Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)
- An AI-generated blog claimed the European Commission fined Apple €2.7B on Aug 5, 2026 and ordered an end to the WebKit requirement; no Commission, Reuters or other primary corroboration was found, so treat as false until shown otherwise. — [bytewavenews (unverified)](https://bytewavenews.lovable.app/blog/eu-forces-apple-open-ios-browser-engines)

### Inferences
- For a US-based student app the EU changes matter only if the developer later wants to sell via Web Distribution or an alternative marketplace in the EU, which now requires no EU entity but carries the 5% CTC plus EU-specific child-safety rules for alternative payments; this is a v2 question. None of the 2026 changes alter the 4.2 calculus or PWA capabilities in the US.

### Gaps
- Apple's EU page no longer states the current status of Home Screen web apps in the EU; the reversal is confirmed only by 2024 secondary reporting. No primary source for whether iOS 26's "every site is a web app" behaviour applies identically in the EU.

# How bring-your-own-key (BYOK) iOS apps get through App Review and how they price, as of October 2026

Research date: 2026-10-14. This extends section 3 of `../AI study app competitors and pricing/app_store_rules.md` (the Sept 2024 thread 763884 rejection and the Pal Chat / OpenCat listings) and does not repeat the guideline quotations already there except where a new clause is needed. Labels: **Confirmed** = quoted from an Apple page, a live App Store listing fetched on the research date, or the iTunes Lookup API; **Developer report** = a named developer's first-hand forum/blog account (reliable about what happened to them, not about Apple policy); **Hearsay** = second-hand, aggregator or review-site claim.

Headline: after sweeping Apple Developer Forums, Hacker News (Algolia API, three queries, zero hits), the App Store, developer blogs and search-engine-visible Reddit/Mastodon/X, I found **no live, verifiable iOS app that is paid-upfront and requires a user-supplied key**, and **no written Apple rejection text about API keys newer than the Sept 2024 thread**. Every BYOK app I could verify live on 2026-10-14 is a free download; the ones that charge do it with a one-time non-consumable (Pal Chat Pro $6.99, OpenCat Pro $9.99, AI Notes Pro $9.99, Geeps Plus $14.99, Offline AI Premium $19.99, Lori's one-time "own key" unlock) or a subscription (Cumbersome, Chatbox). Two live apps (Offline AI, Lori) make the BYOK mode itself the thing the one-time IAP unlocks, which is literally the "make this unlocked content available as in-app purchases" remedy the 2024 reviewer proposed. The cleanest structure for a $2.99-4.99 one-time price is therefore: free download, non-consumable IAP, with the key framed as connecting the user's own Google account and the app useful without it.

### Inferences
- The population of verified BYOK apps is 100% free-to-download. Combined with the single written rejection of a paid-upfront BYOK app (thread 763884), the absence of any verified paid-upfront BYOK survivor is the strongest signal in this research: whatever Apple's policy "is", the market has converged on free + IAP.
- Three distinct monetisation shapes coexist and all pass review: (a) key required, one-time IAP for non-AI conveniences (Geeps, AI Notes, OpenCat Pro, probably Pal Chat); (b) one-time IAP that unlocks the BYOK mode itself (Offline AI, Lori); (c) nothing charged (FlashcardGPT, MealTime, Daotuan, Q.AT). Shape (b) is the one that maps directly onto the 2024 reviewer's remedy language.
- Several BYOK apps self-rate 18+ (Pal Chat, OpenCat, Geeps, Cumbersome, Offline AI) while others are 4+ (FlashcardGPT, MealTime, Daotuan, AI Notes). Apple is not forcing a rating on BYOK as such; the 18+ ones are chat clients that can emit anything, the 4+ ones are narrow tools. A study app with constrained prompts is closer to the latter, subject to the Google 18+ terms issue already covered in the prior note.
- Renaming to "Enchanted Developers Only" and filing BYOK clients under Developer Tools (Enchanted, Geeps) looks like a deliberate positioning tactic: "this is a tool for people who already have keys", which sidesteps 4.2 and the consumer framing. It is also an Apple-acceptable way to signal that the key is a prerequisite, not an unlock.

### Gaps
- No live paid-upfront BYOK iOS app could be verified. LLM Chat Client ($1.99) may have been one; its removal reason is unknown.
- Pal Chat Pro's IAP type (non-consumable vs monthly) is not stated on the listing or anywhere I could reach.
- Lori's IAP names and prices were not fetched (404).
- Hollo, Jan, Msty, Raycast, Kerlig, MacGPT: no iOS App Store presence found; Mac-side distribution not verified.

## 2. Documented rejections 2023-2026 tied to API keys (3.1.1, 3.1.3, 2.1, 5.2.2, 4.2): reviewer text, developer change, outcome

### Takeaway
Only one written rejection names API keys (Sept 2024, thread 763884) and its outcome is still unpublished; two older threads (2018 "Lesson ID" codes, 2022-23 product keys) show the same 3.1.1 "mechanisms other than in-app purchase" template and show that reviewers do not engage with 3.1.3(b) arguments. The only 2025-2026 threads are a July 2025 case where reviewers repeatedly insisted an on-device CoreML app was "an OpenAI wrapper" (guideline not disclosed) and a Feb 2026 2.1 request for access to a self-hosted-API app. Hacker News has zero matching comments; Reddit/Mastodon/X surfaced nothing through search engines.

### Inferences
- The 2018, 2022-23 and 2024 rejections use one template: "unlocks or enables additional functionality with mechanisms other than in-app purchase". Reviewers apply it whenever a typed string gates functionality, regardless of whether money changed hands or whether the string is the developer's or a third party's. The 2018 case (free app, free content) shows the template fires even with zero commerce; the 2024 case shows it fires on a third-party key.
- The resolution path that developers actually report working is substitution of the "code" with something that does not look like a licence key: a username/password login (713044, confirmed by an Apple Support call per the developer), or a web account (109627 advice). For a BYOK app the analogue is to present the key field as "Sign in to your Google AI Studio account" / "Connect your Google account" rather than "Enter key to unlock".
- No developer in any thread reports winning the 3.1.3(b) argument; two report the reviewer ignored it outright. Do not plan on 3.1.3(b) as the primary defence (see section 3).
- The 2025 "OpenAI wrapper" insistence shows reviewers pattern-match on the shape of the app, not the billing. A study app whose core loop is "upload notes -> AI output" will be read as a wrapper unless the non-AI study loop (SRS, manual cards, podcasts of existing audio) is visible first.

### Gaps
- Reddit (r/iOSProgramming, r/iOSapps, r/SideProject) is not fetchable from this environment and no search engine surfaced a matching thread; this corpus remains unchecked.
- No Mastodon or X post about a BYOK rejection was found via search; direct search on those platforms was not possible.
- The 763884 developer (curtisduggan) never posted an outcome; whether the app shipped (and how) is unknown.

## 3. Can 3.1.3(b) Multiplatform Services or 3.1.3(a) Reader Apps cover a user-supplied API key? What does 3.1.1 "unlock features or functionality" reach?

### Takeaway
On the text, neither clause fits: 3.1.3(b) covers items "acquired in your app on other platforms or your web site" and requires those items to "also [be] available as in-app purchases", and 3.1.3(a) is an enumerated list of media types ("magazines, newspapers, books, audio, music, and video"). Developers who tried the 3.1.3(b) argument for product keys report reviewers refused to engage; no one has reported trying it for a third-party API key. The practical defence is not a 3.1.3 carve-out but showing that the key does not "unlock" anything the app sells: the app is complete without it and the key is a connection to a service the user already holds.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed rule, 3.1.3(a) as of 2026-10-14 (verbatim): "Apps may allow a user to access previously purchased content or content subscriptions (specifically: magazines, newspapers, books, audio, music, and video). Reader apps may offer account creation for free tiers, and account management functionality for existing customers. Reader app developers may apply for the External Link Account Entitlement to provide an informational link in their app to a web site the developer owns or maintains responsibility for in order to create or manage an account. This entitlement is not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps." (The 2018 forum copy of 3.1.3(a) still listed "access to professional databases, VoIP, cloud storage, and approved services such as classroom management apps"; those words are gone from the current text.) — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); 2018 wording via [thread 109627](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/109627)
- Confirmed rule, 3.1.3(b) (verbatim): "Apps that operate across multiple platforms may allow users to access content, subscriptions, or features they have acquired in your app on other platforms or your web site, including consumable items in multi-platform games, provided those items are also available as in-app purchases within the app." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 3.1.3(f) (verbatim): "Free apps acting as a stand-alone companion to a paid web based tool (i.e. VoIP, Cloud Storage, Email Services, Web Hosting) do not need to use in-app purchase, provided there is no purchasing inside the app, or calls to action for purchase outside of the app." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule, 3.1.1 first bullet (verbatim, unchanged from the prior note): "...Apps may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as license keys, augmented reality markers, QR codes, cryptocurrencies and cryptocurrency wallets, etc." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Developer report: the only forum cases arguing 3.1.3(b) (713044) involved the developer's own product keys and ended with "we were unilaterally told that product keys were not allowed"; MarkMiles: "what's stated in 3.1.3 is in contrast with what's in 3.1.3(b). On one hand they say that you can allow unlocking features purchased outside the app, on the other hand they block any method you could use to access this content... the only use of the word 'et cetera' means 'everything else'". — [Apple Developer Forums 713044](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/713044)
- Precedent for the "companion to a service the user already pays elsewhere" shape: Obsidian (free, Sync paid off-store, no IAP listed, live) and Enchanted (free, requires the user's own Ollama server). — [Obsidian listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/obsidian-connected-notes/id1557175442); [Enchanted via Lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6474268307&country=us) (Confirmed listings; the 3.1.3(f) characterisation is mine)
- No Apple statement, forum reply, or developer report exists that Apple has accepted a 3.1.3(a) or 3.1.3(b) framing for a third-party API key. Thread 710314 asked and got zero replies. — [Apple Developer Forums 710314](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/710314)

### Inferences
- 3.1.3(b) is a poor fit on three counts: Google's AI Studio is not "your app on other platforms or your web site"; a Gemini key is not an item "acquired in your app"; and the proviso requires the same item to be sold as IAP, which would mean selling Gemini access by IAP, defeating BYOK. 3.1.3(a) does not list software/AI services. Do not lead with either in an appeal; cite them only as evidence that Apple accepts "access what you already have elsewhere" as a principle.
- The closest textual home is 3.1.3(f) (free stand-alone companion to a paid web-based tool, no purchasing or calls to action in-app). It explicitly presumes a *free* app, which is another reason the paid-upfront + key model is structurally weaker than free + IAP: a free app with an optional IAP for non-AI features, where the AI part is a companion to the user's Google AI Studio account, sits inside both 3.1.1 (the IAP is Apple's) and the 3.1.3(f) spirit (the Google side has no in-app purchase or call to action).
- The operative question under 3.1.1 is whether the key "unlocks" something. Apps that pass (Geeps: "An API key is required to use Geeps"; FlashcardGPT: "requires the user's own API Key") present the key as a *prerequisite* for the whole product, not a tier. The 2024 rejected app presented it the same way but was paid; the difference that remains is money changing hands for an app whose value then depends on a non-Apple purchase. Keep the key as a connection ("Connect Google AI Studio"), never as an unlock, and never show the key screen as a paywall-shaped interstitial.

### Gaps
- No case of Apple accepting or rejecting a 3.1.3(a)/(b)/(f) argument specifically about third-party API keys was found.
- Apple's internal reviewer guidance on "mechanisms" is not public.

## 4. Does Apple treat "paid download + requires key" differently from "free download + IAP + requires key"? Evidence beyond the 2024 thread

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is still a single data point (763884), but the indirect evidence is now stronger: every verifiable BYOK app that charges (seven apps) does it through IAP on a free download, the only paid-upfront app that search engines associated with BYOK (LLM Chat Client, $1.99) is no longer resolvable on any storefront, and two live apps (Offline AI, Lori) gate BYOK itself behind a non-consumable, which is what the 2024 reviewer said to do. No consultant or forum source in 2025-2026 states a rule either way.

### Cited Findings
- 2024 reviewer remedy (verbatim, from the prior note): "You may also consider making this unlocked content available to your users as in-app purchases." — [Apple Developer Forums 763884](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/763884)

No subscription."), OpenCat Pro ($9.99 unlocks Keyboard Extension, iCloud Sync, Siri, TTS), Pal Chat Pro ($6.99). — listings cited in section 1
- Live free, no-IAP BYOK approvals in 2023 (FlashcardGPT, Q.AT), 2025 (Enchanted update) and 2026 (MealTime, released 2026-03-05, updated 2026-09-24; Daotuan Chat). — listings cited in section 1
- Paid-upfront BYOK: zero verified live examples; LLM Chat Client (id6757767087, "$1.99", OpenAI/Anthropic/Ollama) returned no Lookup result in US/GB/IN on 2026-10-14. — [iTunes Lookup US](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6757767087&country=us) (Confirmed null)
- Apple's 2.1(a) and 4.2 text already give a reviewer two independent reasons to reject a paid app that is empty without a key (incomplete bundle / not useful). ### Inferences
- Apple has no written rule distinguishing the two models, but the enforcement pattern plus the market outcome point the same way: paid-upfront + key is the only BYOK configuration with a written rejection and no verified survivor. Free + non-consumable + key is the configuration with the most verified survivors (at least six) and no written rejection. For a $2.99-4.99 target the revenue is identical (Apple's cut is the same 15% under the Small Business Program for both paid apps and IAP), so there is no financial reason to prefer paid-upfront.
- A free download also unlocks the standard conversion levers (trial of the manual study features, a hard paywall only on the AI "Supporter" or "Pro" unlock) and avoids the 2.1(a)/4.2 "empty paid app" problem because the reviewer sees working non-AI features before any key.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 developer has published a paid-upfront BYOK rejection or approval; the 2024 thread remains the only direct evidence.
- App-review consultants' pages found do not address BYOK at all.

## 5. Review-notes practice: do developers supply a working API key in App Review notes, and does Apple accept it under 2.1(a)?

### Takeaway
Apple's published guidance says to put demo credentials and any "authentication code" in the Notes field and allows a demonstration mode; it says nothing about third-party API keys. No developer report (positive or negative) of supplying an AI provider key in review notes was found. The one 2026 analogue (self-hosted Pi-hole API, thread 815919) shows Apple does issue 2.1 rejections when it cannot reach the backend and offers only the standard clarification/call/appeal channels.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed, Apple App Review page (as summarised in search results): "If your app has multiple account types (such as admin and general users), use the Notes field to provide additional demo account credentials for each account type. If your app requires an authentication code in addition to the login credentials, provide the code in advance in the Notes field." Apps can also "offer a demonstration mode with demo data". — [Apple App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review)
- Confirmed rule 2.1(a): demo account info required for logins; built-in demo mode only "with prior approval by Apple" (quoted in the prior note). — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Developer report, Feb 2026: rejection under 2.1 "with a request to provide access information so the app, domain, and live query features can be reviewed" for an app that only talks to the user's own Pi-hole; Apple's reply listed only "Reply to the message from App Review... request clarification", "Request a call with an Apple representative", "Submit an appeal to the App Review Board". No outcome. — [Apple Developer Forums 815919](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/815919)
- Developer report (older, App Store Connect API keys, not AI): a developer "created an API key specifically for the app reviewers" because username/password was not available; this is the only "key in review notes" report surfaced and it concerns Apple's own API. — [RevenueCat ultimate guide to rejections](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/the-ultimate-guide-to-app-store-rejections) (Hearsay)
- Consultant advice (Hearsay): "For a BYOK app, that could mean a demo mode or sample content that works without a key... supply a working test key in the App Review notes." — [search summary of RevenueCat/Runway guides](https://www.runway.team/blog/submitting-app-store-avoiding-rejections)
- No forum thread was found in which a reviewer refused to use a supplied third-party key, and none in which a reviewer confirmed using one. Searches: `site:developer.apple.com/forums reviewer "API key" "review notes"` and variants. (Confirmed null)

### Inferences
- Putting a funded (paid-tier) Gemini key in the Notes field is consistent with Apple's "authentication code" instruction and costs nothing; it is the community default and there is no report of it backfiring. Pair it with a one-line explanation that the key belongs to the developer and is for review only, so the reviewer does not read it as the app shipping a key.
- Because 2.1(a) demo mode needs "prior approval by Apple", a BYOK app should not rely on a hidden demo switch; instead make the non-AI features real and let the reviewer key exercise the AI path.
- Thread 815919 is a warning that if the reviewer cannot exercise the AI path they will reject under 2.1 rather than wave it through; a dead key (rate-limited free tier, revoked, wrong region) will produce that rejection.

### Gaps
- No confirmed report, positive or negative, of an AI-provider API key supplied in review notes.
- Whether App Review's own network/region can reach the Gemini API without issue is unverified (Google's regions page lists the US as available, so this is likely fine).

## 6. The May 2025 US anti-steering change and the October 2026 state; linking to aistudio.google.com or to a developer's own licence page; EU terms

### Takeaway
The US-storefront exemption added on May 2, 2025 is still in the live guidelines (3.1.1(a), 3.1.3, 3.1.3(a)) and Apple's attempt to undo it has not succeeded: the Ninth Circuit affirmed the contempt finding on December 11, 2025 (while saying Apple may charge "limited" cost-tied fees on linked transactions), the Supreme Court refused a stay on May 6, 2026, then granted certiorari on June 30, 2026 with Apple's merits brief filed September 14, 2026, so the rule could change in 2027. A link to aistudio.google.com is not a purchase link anyway (a 2026-approved app, MealTime, tells users "Google's free tier needs only a Google account, no card"), so 3.1.1 anti-steering does not reach it in any storefront. In the EU, new terms announced August 18, 2026 (effective October 1, 2026) set 26% IAP / 20% alternative payments / 15% link-out commissions and a 5% Core Technology Commission for web/marketplace distribution.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed, live guideline text 2026-10-14: 3.1.1(a) "These entitlements are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps"; 3.1.3 intro "...except for apps on the United States storefront and as set forth in 3.1.1(a) and 3.1.3(a)"; StoreKit External Purchase Link Entitlement paragraph: "In all other storefronts, except for the United States storefront, where this prohibition does not apply, apps and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Ninth Circuit, December 11, 2025 (reported by Epic's counsel Cravath on Dec 22, 2025): affirmed the civil-contempt finding, "the injunction's ban on limiting the design or placement of steering links", and denial of Apple's request to vacate or modify; "although Apple should be allowed to charge some fees on linked transactions... those should be limited and tied to the costs of any services Apple provides in facilitating steering links"; remanded for further proceedings. — [Cravath](https://www.cravath.com/news-insights/epic-games-ninth-circuit-win-affirming-civil-contempt-finding.html)
- Supreme Court declined to pause the contempt order (CNBC, May 6, 2026; page returned 403 to fetch, headline and date from search). Cert petition filed May 22, 2026, docketed No. 25-1311; Oyez lists "Granted Jun 30, 2026"; Apple's merits opening brief dated 2026-09-14 is on the Supreme Court docket. Questions presented per the petition: "when can an injunction obtained by a single plaintiff bind an entire developer ecosystem, and can civil contempt lie for conduct that violates the 'spirit' but not the express text of an order". — [CNBC May 6, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/06/supreme-court-declines-to-pause-order-holding-apple-in-contempt-in-epic-games-lawsuit.html); [Oyez 25-1311](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2026/25-1311); [Apple merits brief PDF, Sept 14, 2026](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-1311/424150/20260914154510009_2026-09-14%20No.%2025-1311%20Apple-Epic%20Merits%20Opening%20Brief.pdf); [Sigma Law Group, May 26, 2026](https://sigmalawgroup.com/blog/2026-05-26-apple-epic-cert/) (dates from search snippets; Oyez page did not render)
- AppleInsider (April 29, 2026): on commissions for US external links, "Apple and Epic must decide on what would be acceptable, but that hasn't happened yet." — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/04/29/app-store-policy-must-change-as-epic-convinces-us-circuit-court-to-reverse-stay) (page 403 to fetch; from search snippet)
- Conflicting claim (Hearsay): a May 16, 2026 implementation guide says US external purchases "cost 12% (Small Business) or 27%" within an attribution window and that developers must "formally request the StoreKit External Purchase Link entitlement". This contradicts the live guideline text ("entitlements are not required... United States storefront") and the Ninth Circuit's "limited and tied to the costs" language; the 12%/27% figures are the pre-injunction 2024 US entitlement rates. Treat as unreliable for the US; its non-US statement ("the same external-link UI in a globally distributed app is still a 3.1.1 violation in Japan, Canada, Australia, the UK, and most other regions") matches the guideline text. — [stora.sh guide](https://stora.sh/blog/2026-05-16-apple-app-store-external-purchase-links-implementation-guide)
- Live precedent for pointing users at Google for a free key (Confirmed): MealTime (approved March 2026, updated Sept 2026): "Bring your own API key: MealTime never charges for AI"; "Google's free tier needs only a Google account, no card". — [iTunes Lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6758350120&country=us)
- EU (Confirmed via reporting): terms announced August 18, 2026, effective October 1, 2026: Core Technology Fee replaced by a 5% Core Technology Commission for web distribution/alternative marketplaces; App Store apps pay 26% with Apple IAP, 20% with alternative payment processing, 15% for link-out to web purchase; 15%/10% for Small Business Program and partner programs; Apple IAP may now be offered alongside alternative payments in the EU. — [mjtsai, Aug 18, 2026](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2026/08/18/new-eu-app-store-terms-to-comply-with-dma/); [Anadolu Agency](https://aa.com.tr/en/science-technology/apple-revamps-eu-app-store-fees-to-resolve-regulatory-dispute/4031035); [Phiture](https://phiture.com/blog/apple-new-app-store-terms-2026/)

### Inferences
- A "Get a free key at aistudio.google.com" button is informational, not a "purchasing mechanism", in every storefront; MealTime's approved copy is a usable template. Avoid pairing it with anything that reads as a price ("upgrade to a paid key for more"), especially outside the US.
- Selling a licence on the developer's own website and linking to it from the US app is now legal under the guidelines without an entitlement, and the commission Apple may collect is unsettled (court said "limited and tied to costs"; Apple says it is still deciding). For a $2.99-4.99 product the operational cost of web checkout plus licence validation (which would itself be a "license key" under 3.1.1 for non-US users) exceeds the 15% Small Business Program cut; it is not worth it at this price point and reintroduces the exact mechanism the 2018/2022 rejections targeted.
- Cert was granted on judicial-power questions, not on whether links are allowed; a 2027 reversal could restore Apple's ability to restrict US links, but a non-consumable IAP is unaffected either way.

### Gaps
- The CNBC, AppleInsider and Oyez pages could not be fetched (403/blank template); dates and quotes above come from search-result snippets and the Cravath page.
- Whether Apple currently collects any commission on US external links in practice, and at what rate, is unresolved in the sources.

## 7. One-time pricing structure in 2026: non-consumable IAP vs paid download; benchmark data; tips and "pay what you want"

### Takeaway
Industry data in 2026 is about subscriptions; the lifetime/one-time numbers that exist say roughly one in four subscription apps also sells a lifetime plan, lifetime is priced at 2x-12x the annual price, and hard-paywall download-to-paid conversion is about 10.7% versus 2.1% for freemium. The live BYOK apps cluster their one-time unlocks at $6.99-$19.99 (median $9.99) and the paid-upfront local-LLM apps at $4.99-$5.99, so $2.99-4.99 would be the cheapest one-time price in the category. Tips to the developer must go through IAP (3.2.1(vii) exempts only person-to-person gifts where 100% reaches the receiver); a multi-tier consumable tip jar is the accepted "pay what you want" shape and is documented live in Pedometer++.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 (Education cut; covers 2025 data through Feb 2026): 23.2% of apps offer subscriptions plus a lifetime plan; 2.5% offer subscriptions + consumables + lifetime; "one in four apps offers a lifetime plan" (contributor quote); Photo & Video highest lifetime-combo usage at 33.0%; among plans sold, "Lifetime/Other is 4-18% by category"; lifetime-dominant apps have median revenue per install of $0.19 at D14 and $0.24 at D60; download-to-paid at D35 median 2.0% overall, hard paywall 10.7% (top quartile >20%, top decile 38.7%), freemium 2.1%; Education has the highest yearly median price ($44.99), 28.5% of conversions on Day 0, $0.30 median D14 RPI; monthly median $8 (most common $10), yearly median $34.80 (most common $30). Note: the page gives inconsistent figures in places (e.g. North America D35 2.6% vs 2.8%). — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)
- RevenueCat lifetime guide (Daphne Tideman, Nov 12, 2025): lifetime "often priced at 5-12x the annual subscription", observed range "from 2x annual pricing to nearly 12x"; examples Calm $79 annual / $399 lifetime (5.0x), Moonly $28.98 / $59.98 (2.1x), Placify $14.99 / $49.99 (3.3x), Waking Up $129.99 / $1,500 (11.5x); lifetime is "the second most popular monetization form for most categories"; outside leading categories adoption "hovers around 18-24% of apps"; Education cited as a category where lifetime is popular "because users see clear long-term value"; "if in doubt, I'd always recommend going higher rather than lower"; no conversion statistics given. — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/lifetime-subscriptions)
- AppsFlyer State of App Monetization 2026: North America converts 11.14% of installs to one-time buyers and 5.08% to repeat buyers (all one-time purchases, not lifetime plans specifically). — [AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/resources/reports/app-marketing-monetization-report/) (from search snippet)
- Airbridge citing Adapty: one-time/lifetime purchases grew from 6.4% to 10.3% of app monetization models between 2023 and 2025. — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/should-you-offer-a-lifetime-subscription) (Hearsay; not verified against Adapty)
- Secondary price ranges: non-consumable IAP "$4.99-$29.99 per purchase" (comparison blogs; unsourced). — [search summary](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/subscription-vs-one-time-purchase-app) (Hearsay)
- Live price points in the BYOK/AI-client category on 2026-10-14 (Confirmed, section 1): paid-upfront no-key: Private LLM $4.99, OfflineLLM $5.99; one-time unlocks on free BYOK apps: Pal Chat Pro $6.99, OpenCat Pro $9.99, AI Notes Summary Pro $9.99, Geeps Plus $14.99, Offline AI Premium $19.99; subscriptions: Cumbersome $4.99/mo or $39.99/yr, OpenCat Cloud AI $3.99/$24.99.
- Confirmed rule 3.2.1(vii) (verbatim): "Apps may enable individual users to give a monetary gift to another individual without using in-app purchase, provided that (a) the gift is a completely optional choice by the giver, and (b) 100% of the funds go to the receiver of the gift. However, a gift that is connected to or associated at any point in time with receiving digital content or services must use in-app purchase." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Confirmed rule 3.1.1 (2017 wording, still present): "Apps may use in-app purchase currencies to enable customers to 'tip' digital content providers in the app." — [Engadget, June 9, 2017](https://www.engadget.com/2017-06-09-apple-tips-ios-apps.html); [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Tip-jar practice (Developer reports/vendor guides): RevenueCat and Nami both say consumables are "perfect for tips"; Becky Hansmeyer notes consumables "cannot be restored" and that Pedometer++ ships "a 3-tiered tip jar that will disable advertisements at any tier"; in a RevenueCat case study, No Ads 2048 found "players started choosing higher-value tips more often when given several options"; a forum thread reports consumable tip IAPs rejected under 3.1.1 "for a change in IAP type" with no resolution. — [RevenueCat tip jar guide](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/building-a-tip-jar-feature-with-revenuecat); [Nami](https://namiml.com/blog/let-your-fans-support-your-app-with-a-tip-jar); [Becky Hansmeyer](https://www.beckyhansmeyer.com/tag/pricing/); [Apple Developer Forums 770266](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/770266); [thread 116100](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/116100)

### Inferences
- Non-consumable IAP on a free download dominates paid-upfront for this product: identical 15% commission under the Small Business Program, Family Sharing available for non-consumables, promo codes and introductory flows available, the 4.2/2.1 "empty paid app" risk removed, and the 3.1.1 BYOK exposure reduced (section 4). The only thing paid-upfront buys is the absence of a paywall screen, which Apple's own data says costs conversion anyway (hard paywall 10.7% vs freemium 2.1% download-to-paid; a hard paywall on the AI/Supporter unlock is the configuration RevenueCat's numbers favour).
- $2.99-4.99 is below every verified one-time unlock in the category ($6.99-$19.99) and at the bottom of the paid-upfront local-LLM range ($4.99-5.99). RevenueCat's "go higher rather than lower" plus the category floor argue for $4.99 as the base one-time price, with $2.99 reserved for a launch or student promo via offer codes rather than as list price.
- "Pay what you want" is implementable only as a tiered consumable tip jar (3 tiers, e.g. $1.99/$4.99/$9.99) and tips cannot be restored, so anything that must persist across devices (the Supporter unlock) must be a non-consumable. The common hybrid: one non-consumable "Supporter" that persists, plus consumable tips that do not unlock anything (Pedometer++ shows tips can also remove ads, but that is a stretch toward "digital content" and not needed here).
- Never let a tip be "connected to or associated at any point in time with receiving digital content or services" outside IAP (3.2.1(vii)); a web donation link that unlocks anything would violate 3.1.1 outside the US.

### Gaps
- No 2026 dataset reports median *price* of lifetime/one-time plans; RevenueCat's report gives shares and RPI, not prices. Superwall and Appfigures published nothing on lifetime pricing that I could find.
- No conversion benchmark specific to $2.99-4.99 non-consumables in Education exists in the sources.
- The 2026 RevenueCat PDF (330+ pages) was not opened; the Education web cut was used.

## 8. Would a free download with a non-consumable "Supporter" unlock that gates only non-AI conveniences (themes, unlimited kits, export) while AI runs on the user's key satisfy 3.1.1? Any precedent of that exact split?

### Takeaway
Yes, with live precedent. OpenCat is the exact split: free app, BYOK (and local) AI, and a $9.99 one-time "OpenCat Pro" that unlocks only non-AI conveniences (keyboard extension, iCloud sync, Siri, text-to-speech, team join). Geeps ("An API key is required to use Geeps"; $14.99 one-time Plus for "some tools and features") and AI Notes BYOK ($9.99 one-time Summary Pro) are the same shape with less detail. The alternative split, where the one-time IAP unlocks the BYOK mode itself (Offline AI $19.99, Lori), also has live precedent and matches the 2024 reviewer's remedy text more literally; it is safer against 3.1.1 but means the app's AI features are IAP-gated, which is what the user asked to avoid for a $2.99-4.99 price.

### Cited Findings
- OpenCat (Confirmed): Free tier "Prompt Library, Markdown Rendering, Multiple Models"; "OpenCat Pro: $9.99, one-time purchase" unlocks "Keyboard Extension, iCloud Sync, Siri Integration, Text to Speech, Join Team" and "Additional Pro features"; "BYOK — use your own API Key, including Coding Plan"; v26.10.1 live Oct 2026. — [opencat.app](https://opencat.app/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/opencat-chat-with-ai-bot/id6445999201)
- Geeps (Confirmed): "An API key is required to use Geeps."; "Geeps Plus $14.99... a one-time upgrade, not a subscription... only some tools and features require it"; v2.0.1 Oct 4. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/geeps-ai-chat-byok/id6476961115)
- AI Notes: BYOK Transcription (Confirmed): BYOK; "AI Summary Pro $9.99"; "One-time purchase. No subscription. No account required." — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ai-notes-byok-transcription/id6749819451)
- Pal Chat (Confirmed listing; Hearsay on gating): free, key required, "Pal Chat Pro $6.99"; third-party summary says Pro "gate[s] features behind a paywall". — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pal-chat-ai-chat-client/id6447545085); [topai.tools](https://topai.tools/t/pal-chat)
- Inverse split, IAP unlocks BYOK (Confirmed): Offline AI "one-time Premium unlock for cloud AI providers" $19.99; Lori "bring their own OpenAI key... after one non-subscription purchase". — [Offline AI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/offline-ai-private-local-gpt/id6748595214); [Lori snippet](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ai-chat-companion-lori/id6446085163)
- Rule basis (Confirmed): 3.1.1 requires IAP "to unlock features or functionality within your app"; the Supporter unlock is IAP, so it complies by construction; the only remaining question is whether the *key* is a second unlock mechanism, which section 3 addresses. — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Cosmetic/convenience unlocks (themes, icons) as the paid layer over a free core is a common indie pattern (e.g. PopClip forum discussion of a "supporter tier" and supporter icon; Pedometer++ tip tiers) but no Apple text addresses it specifically. — [PopClip forum](https://forum.popclip.app/t/nick-asks-whats-your-feedback-on-this-in-app-purchase/898); [Becky Hansmeyer](https://www.beckyhansmeyer.com/tag/pricing/) (Hearsay)

### Inferences
- The proposed structure (free download; non-consumable "Supporter" $4.99 gating themes, unlimited kits, export, sync extras; AI on the user's key, usable without paying) is the OpenCat/Geeps shape and has been passing review continuously since 2023. The design choices those apps share, and which the rejected 2024 app lacked: free download; key described as "required"/"your own" in the listing (never "unlock"); a visible free tier that does something; the paid item is an Apple IAP with a concrete non-AI feature list.
- Keep the Supporter unlock free of anything that reads as "more AI" (no "unlimited AI generations", no "faster model") so a reviewer cannot pair the key and the IAP into a two-step unlock of the same functionality. If kits are generated by AI, phrase the limit as storage ("unlimited saved kits"), not generation.
- If a reviewer still cites 3.1.1 on the key, the fallback with precedent is to move the BYOK toggle itself behind the same $4.99 non-consumable (Offline AI/Lori shape). This keeps the price target, needs no new SKU, and matches the reviewer's own remedy wording. Build the paywall so this flip is a config change.
- Review notes should state the split explicitly: "The app is free and fully usable for manual flashcards, spaced repetition and audio playback. AI features connect to the user's own Google AI Studio account (a free key; the app sells no AI). The only purchase is the 'Supporter' non-consumable IAP, which unlocks [list]. A funded test key for App Review: ...". This answers 2.1(a), 2.3.1 and 3.1.1 in one paragraph and mirrors the listing copy of approved apps.

# Ways around the Gemini API terms problem for a consumer iOS study app (BYOK Gemini), as of October 14, 2026

Scope note: this extends section 4 of `research_notes/AI study app competitors and pricing/app_store_rules.md` and does not restate the Gemini API Additional Terms clauses already quoted there (18+, "likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18", "not for consumer use", EEA/UK/CH paid-only, free-tier training). "Confirmed" = read from the primary page on 2026-10-14; "Secondary" = press/blog; "Inference" = my reasoning.

## 1. Google's own sanctioned consumer path: Firebase AI Logic (Gemini Developer API or Vertex/Agent Platform backend), App Check, developer-paid billing, IAP-funded credits

### Takeaway
Firebase AI Logic is Google's supported way to call Gemini from a shipped mobile app (the developer's key stays on Google's proxy, App Check attests each request, per-user rate limits are on by default, and the proxy itself is free). It does not remove the under-18 problem: the Gemini Developer API backend is still "subject to [the Gemini API's] specific terms", and the Vertex/Agent Platform backend is governed by the Google Cloud Service Specific Terms, whose Generative AI section carries the identical sentence forbidding use "as part of a website, Customer Application, or other online service that is directed towards or is likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18." What the Cloud path does remove is the "not for consumer use" sentence (not present in the Cloud terms; Firebase instead asks you to attest your use is "for purposes related to my trade, business, craft, or profession", which a developer selling an app satisfies). Developer-paid inference at Mnemo's measured $1.50-9/month per active student is only viable under an IAP if you pick a Flash-Lite/Haiku-class model and cap usage; on Gemini 3.8 Flash the same token volume costs 1.5-2.5x more today and doubles again on Jan 1, 2027.

### Inferences
- Under-18 exposure is identical on every Google path. The Gemini API Additional Terms (BYOK or Firebase Developer API backend) and the Cloud Service Specific Terms §20(d) (Vertex/Agent Platform backend) use the same "directed towards or is likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18" test, and on the Cloud path a suspected violation is an immediate-suspension ground (§20(f)). Moving to Vertex buys you out of "not for consumer use", out of free-tier training, and into Google's IP indemnity and 30-day-max abuse logging, but not out of the age clause.
- Cost per student per month if the developer pays (assumption: the $1.50-9/month Mnemo figure was measured at Gemini 2.5 Flash-class prices, $0.30 in / $2.50 out; multipliers below are input-price ratio to output-price ratio, so the range brackets any input/output mix):
  - Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite ($0.30/$2.50): 1.0x -> $1.50-9.00
  - Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite ($0.25/$1.50): 0.6-0.83x -> about $0.90-7.50
  - Gemini 3.8 / 3.6 Flash ($0.75/$3.75): 1.5-2.5x -> about $2.25-22.50 now; 3-5x ($4.50-45) from Jan 1, 2027
  - Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite ($0.10/$0.40): 0.16-0.33x -> about $0.25-3.00 (older model; check deprecation)
  - Claude Haiku 5.5 ($0.10/$0.50 for prompts under 100k tokens, see Q3): 0.2-0.33x -> about $0.30-3.00
  - DeepSeek V4.1-Flash peak ($0.30/$1.20): 0.48-1.0x; off-peak ($0.15/$0.60): 0.24-0.5x (see Q3)
  - OpenAI gpt-5.4-nano ($0.20/$1.25): 0.5-0.67x; gpt-5-nano ($0.05/$0.40, deprecated): about 0.16x
  Batch/Flex tiers halve these for non-interactive jobs (deck generation from a PDF is batchable; short-answer grading is not).
- An IAP-funded model therefore works only with a cap. Apple's commission (30%, or 15% in the Small Business Program; not re-verified this session, see https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/) means a $4.99/month subscription nets roughly $4.24 (15%) or $3.49 (30%). That covers the median student on Flash-Lite/Haiku pricing but not the $9 tail on 3.8 Flash, so the design needs either a monthly token/credit allowance (consumable IAP "AI credits" is squarely allowed under 3.1.1 since it is a digital good bought through IAP), or routing bulk generation to Batch and keeping only grading interactive. Firebase's per-user rate limits (on by default) are the enforcement point.
- "Customer Application that has material value independent of the Services" (GCP Terms 1.1) dovetails with Apple 4.2 and with the prior file's advice: the app must be useful without AI.

### Gaps
- Firebase docs do not state which Firebase plan the Agent Platform backend needs; assume Blaze (billing) since the Firebase FM-framework page says it has a "requirement for billing", but this is not a verbatim plan statement.
- No Google page says in words whether the Gemini API Additional Terms' "not for consumer use" sentence applies when the Gemini Developer API is reached through Firebase AI Logic; the Firebase footnote ("subject to their specific terms") implies it does.
- Apple commission percentages were not fetched this session.

## 2. Does Google offer any OAuth / "sign in with Google, use your own quota" consumer path (Gemini app subscriptions, AI Pro/Ultra, Gemini CLI / Code Assist OAuth)?

### Takeaway
No. The one consumer OAuth path that existed (Gemini CLI's "Login with Google" via Gemini Code Assist for individuals, which third-party agent tools had been piggybacking on) was shut off on June 18, 2026 for free individuals and for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers; Google's own announcement says Gemini CLI "will remain accessible via paid Gemini and Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform API keys" and enterprise Code Assist licenses only. Google AI Pro/Ultra subscriptions are not documented as including Gemini API quota (secondary sources; one conflicting unverified claim). There is no "Sign in with Gemini" developer product analogous to OpenAI's Sign in with ChatGPT.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed (Google Developers Blog, May 19, 2026, Dmitry Lyalin and Taylor Mullen): "On June 18, 2026, Gemini CLI and Gemini Code Assist IDE extensions will stop serving requests for Google AI Pro and Ultra" and "those using it free of charge using" Gemini Code Assist for individuals; "your access remains unchanged" for Gemini Code Assist Standard/Enterprise licenses; "Gemini CLI will remain accessible via paid Gemini and Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform API keys"; "Antigravity CLI is available to everyone" from May 19, 2026. The post says nothing about third-party tools using the CLI's OAuth or the Code Assist API. — [An important update: Transitioning Gemini CLI to Antigravity CLI](https://developers.googleblog.com/an-important-update-transitioning-gemini-cli-to-antigravity-cli/)
- Confirmed (Google Cloud docs): Gemini Code Assist release notes / sunset page say the individuals, AI Pro and AI Ultra tiers stopped being served on June 18, 2026 and users should migrate to Antigravity and Antigravity CLI. — [Gemini Code Assist sunset](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/docs/codeassist/sunset); [release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/docs/codeassist/release-notes)
- Secondary: Antigravity CLI is closed source, written in Go, "won't have 1:1 feature parity right out of the gate"; developers on GitHub called the move a "bait and switch". — [Shopifreaks, May 24, 2026](https://www.shopifreaks.com/google-ends-free-gemini-cli-access-for-non-enterprise-users-on-june-18-replacing-it-with-closed-source-antigravity-cli/); [FOSS Force](https://fossforce.com/2026/05/gemini-clis-short-life-and-googles-antigravity-bait-and-switch/); [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2026/05/19/google-moves-gemini-cli-into-antigravity-cli-as-agent-platform-expands.aspx)
- Secondary, Google AI Pro/Ultra and the API: one explainer says the Gemini API and AI Studio "are billed separately by token usage"; Ultra's developer perks are described as $100/month Google Cloud credits via Google Developer Program Premium and "Highest" AI Studio access, not API quota; a TechCabal piece implies Pro/Ultra "give users API credits by default" but does not explain it (unverified, conflicts with the others). — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/google-ai-ultra); [9to5Google, Feb 21, 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/02/21/google-ai-pro-ultra-features/); [TechCabal, June 5, 2026](https://techcabal.com/2026/06/05/google-ai-subscription-plans-explained/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-overhauls-its-ai-subscriptions-at-i-o-2026-with-three-tiers-starting-at-10-a-month/)
- Not found: no report that Cline or any other third-party app was asked by Google to remove a Gemini CLI/Code Assist OAuth provider; the search returned only the CLI shutdown coverage. — [dev.to summary of the shutdown](https://dev.to/owen_fox/gemini-cli-free-tier-shut-down-6-fixes-that-work-2026-26hc)

### Inferences
- Even before June 18, 2026 the Code Assist OAuth flow was a coding-assistant entitlement, not a general Gemini API entitlement, and Google never published terms permitting third-party clients to use it. After June 18 the question is moot: a consumer who signs in with Google has no Gemini quota an app could draw on. The closest Google analogue to OpenAI's "plan usage" simply does not exist as of October 2026.
- The Antigravity CLI (`agy`) uses a Google account login (the user's CLAUDE.md notes it runs on a student plan), but there is no published API or terms allowing a third-party iOS app to reuse that session; treat any attempt as unsupported and likely a terms violation.

### Gaps
- The Gemini CLI authentication docs could not be fetched (GitHub raw 404 at the old path), so the historical quota numbers for "Login with Google" are not quoted here.
- No primary Google page was found stating in words that AI Pro/Ultra exclude API quota; the conclusion rests on the pricing page (which only mentions free tier vs paid tier by Cloud billing) plus secondary sources.

## 3. Other hosted providers: age / consumer clauses, BYOK permission, free tiers, training rules, flash-class prices

### Takeaway
No major provider's terms explicitly bless "end user pastes their own key into a third-party app". The clearest written permission is Anthropic's Claude Code legal page, which says its credential rules do "not restrict how customers provision and manage their own API keys ... provided the resulting usage is billed to the key owner under their agreement with Anthropic ... and is not resold or intermediated" and that each end user "must authenticate with their own Anthropic API key" when a platform hosts Claude Code. Anthropic's Commercial Terms nonetheless say "Services under these Terms are not for consumer use" and have no age clause (the Consumer Terms are 18+). OpenAI's consumer ToU is 13+ with parental permission under 18, but its Business Terms could not be fetched (403) and OpenAI staff have historically said BYOK is not permitted. Groq (18+, "may not resell or lease access to its Account", minors are the customer's legal problem rather than prohibited), OpenRouter (18+, silent on BYOK, no training), Mistral (no explicit age, "buy, sell, or transfer API keys" and "grant any third party access ... without our prior written authorization" prohibited), DeepSeek (minors "shall read in the presence of a legal guardian", "do not share ... your API key with others"), xAI enterprise (18+), Together (13+ with parental consent) round out the table. For cost, Claude Haiku 5.5 ($0.10/$0.50), DeepSeek V4.1-Flash ($0.15-0.30/$0.60-1.20), Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite ($0.25/$1.50) and gpt-5.4-nano ($0.20/$1.25) all keep Mnemo's token volume within $1-9/month.

### Inferences
- Ranked by how comfortable each provider's text is with a BYOK consumer study app for adults: Groq (explicit end-user/Customer Application rights, minors handled as a legal-compliance duty not a ban, 18+ for the key holder) and Anthropic (explicit "billed to the key owner" permission, though Commercial Terms say "not for consumer use" and Consumer Terms are 18+) are the least hostile; OpenRouter and DeepSeek are silent-to-tolerant; Mistral's "grant any third party access ... without our prior written authorization" and "transfer API keys" language is the most BYOK-hostile; OpenAI is unresolved in writing and historically discouraging. None of them permits under-18 key holders except Together (13+ with consent) and arguably DeepSeek (guardian-present reading).
- For under-18 students, no hosted provider offers a clean BYOK path: an under-18 cannot hold a Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter, xAI or Anthropic key at all. A multi-provider BYOK design (as Pal Chat/OpenCat do) spreads the risk but does not solve the age problem.

## 4. On-device models: Apple Foundation Models (iOS 26/27), Private Cloud Compute, Gemini Nano in Chrome, Gemma on iOS via LiteRT-LM / MediaPipe / MLX / llama.cpp

### Takeaway
Apple's on-device Foundation Model is free, needs no key and no provider terms, supports `@Generable` typed output and `Tool` calling, but has a 4,096-token context per session (prompt + instructions + schema + tool definitions + output), 16 supported languages, and runs only on Apple Intelligence hardware (iPhone 15 Pro/Pro Max, iPhone 16 and later, iPhone Air, iPad mini A17 Pro, M1+ iPads/Macs, MacBook Neo). iOS 27 adds `PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel`: a 32K-context server model with reasoning levels, a per-user daily quota (upgradeable via iCloud+), and no cloud API cost for developers in the App Store Small Business Program with under 2 million first-time downloads. Apple's docs position the on-device model for "summarization, entity extraction, text and image understanding, refinement" and say to use PCC or a server model "when you need more reasoning capabilities and context size"; there is no published benchmark on MCQ/distractor quality. Chrome's Gemini Nano Prompt API is not available on Android, iOS or Safari, so it is irrelevant to the iOS app. MediaPipe LLM Inference for iOS is in maintenance-only mode and points to LiteRT-LM Swift; Gemma weights carry no 18+ or consumer clause.

### Inferences
- Fit for Mnemo's three AI jobs:
  - Generating MCQs/flashcards from a lecture PDF: feasible on-device only by chunking. With a 4,096-token window shared with instructions and the `@Generable` schema (which itself costs tokens), a realistic chunk is roughly 1,500-2,500 words of lecture text yielding a handful of items per session; a 30-page PDF becomes 10-20 sequential sessions. Quality for distractors is unproven (no benchmark found); a general-purpose study-tools piece argues MCQ distractor writing wants 30B-70B-class models, which is a claim about LLMs generally, not a test of Apple's 3B model. PCC (32K, reasoning) is the natural free upgrade on iOS 27 within Apple's daily quota.
  - Grading short answers against a rubric: a classification/extraction-style task squarely inside Apple's stated sweet spot; one session per answer fits easily in 4K; this is the most promising "core feature works without a key" candidate.
  - Podcast/TTS and long-document chat: out of scope for the on-device model (no audio generation; 4K context).
- Reach: the device list excludes every non-Pro iPhone before the 16 and all A-series iPads except the mini A17 Pro, so a meaningful fraction of students in 2026 cannot run it; the app needs a non-AI fallback or a cloud tier regardless.
- Terms: Apple publishes no separate terms of service for Foundation Models beyond the developer agreement, and the Gemma licence has no age bar, so an on-device tier has no 5.2.2 exposure. PCC's eligibility (Small Business Program, under 2M downloads) fits an indie app; losing eligibility gives 6 months to migrate.
- Gemini Nano/Chrome is irrelevant for the Capacitor iOS build (Safari/WKWebView) and only matters for the web PWA on desktop Chrome with a 22 GB download, i.e. not a "core features work without a key" path.

## 5. Hybrid designs other apps use in 2025-2026: on-device free tier + optional BYOK; developer-hosted proxy with IAP credits; "Sign in with ChatGPT" / "Sign in with Claude" entitlement flows

### Takeaway
Three patterns are live. (a) Apple's Foundation Models framework now accepts third-party `LanguageModel` adapters, and Firebase ships a `GeminiLanguageModel` (preview, iOS 27, Firebase SDK 12.19+) so the same `LanguageModelSession` code can run on-device or on Gemini per call, with App Check attestation and the developer's Firebase project paying; OpenCat already mixes BYOK, a cloud IAP tier, local models and Apple Foundation Models (prior file). ## 6. Age gating: is an 18+ App Store rating plus the Declared Age Range API enough under Google's "likely to be accessed by" standard? Any Google statement or enforcement on BYOK consumer apps since 2024?

### Takeaway
Google has published nothing interpreting "likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18" and its forum staff explicitly refuse to: at least eight developer threads between May and October 2026 asking exactly Mnemo's question (adult consumer iOS BYOK, high-school study app BYOK, all-ages iOS app with user keys, kids' learning apps) received either no reply or the stock answer "We can't provide specific legal guidance on this forum." (Sept 30, 2026). No public enforcement action against Pal Chat, OpenCat or similar BYOK clients was found. Apple's Declared Age Range API (iOS 26+, entitlement) lets you gate on an 18 threshold and tells you whether the declaration was self-declared, guardian-declared or "confirmed" (credit card / government ID, iOS 26.5+), and in regulated regions the range is supplied automatically and cannot be declined; Apple warns the data is declared, not verified, and that you are "solely responsible for ensuring compliance." Whether that satisfies Google is a judgment call nobody at Google will make in public; Groq's analogous clause shows how a provider can draft the same idea as a compliance duty rather than a ban, which Google chose not to do.

### Inferences
- The strongest defensible posture for a Gemini (BYOK or Firebase) leg is cumulative: App Store age rating 18+ (prior file: "higher minimum age" option), Declared Age Range gate at 18 with the Gemini features hidden (not just disabled) below 18 or on `declinedSharing`, marketing copy aimed at university/adult learners rather than "high school", and in-app text that the Gemini key holder must be 18+ per Google's terms. It is still not a written Google authorization, and Google will not give one in public.
- Because the on-device Apple model and PCC carry no age clause, an age-gated architecture can let under-18 students use the AI features on-device and reserve Gemini/other hosted providers for the 18+ cohort; that is the only design found that serves high-schoolers without relying on Google's silence.
- Treat an under-18 who pastes a Gemini key as Google's contractual breach (they accepted the Additional Terms when creating the key), but the "API Client ... likely to be accessed by" sentence binds the developer independently, so gating is the developer's own obligation, not just a notice to the user.
